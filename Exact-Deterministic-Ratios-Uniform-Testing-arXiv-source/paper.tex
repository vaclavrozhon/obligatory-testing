\begin{abstract}
A machine faces many jobs whose lengths are hidden.  Spending one unit of
time to inspect a job may reveal a short job that should be finished now, or
it may reveal nothing useful while every other job waits.  When should the
machine keep looking, and when should it start working?

We study this question for one machine and total completion time.  We
consider four natural rules: every job must be tested; a unit-time revealing
optimization can replace a raw time $u$ by a shorter revealed length; an
untested job can be run blindly for its true hidden length; or a unit-time
optimization can shorten it without revealing the new length.  Together these
models separate the two possible benefits of spending
one unit before execution: learning which job to do next and making the job
faster.

We give exact asymptotic answers for these models, both as competitive ratios
and as instance-optimal guarantees.  The resulting algorithms are often
quite simple, which may make them useful in practice.

\end{abstract}

obligatory testing was obtained with the Bolzano open-source multi-agent
research system~\cite{GrebikEtAl2026Bolzano}.  That preliminary result
motivated the present project.  Most of the subsequent conjecture generation,
proof development, counterexample search, exposition, and Lean formalization
was carried out with substantial assistance from OpenAI's GPT-5.6 through
interactive Codex sessions.  In particular, the randomized revealing-optimization
curve was developed in a separate model run and then integrated with the
instance-optimal framework.  The author selected the research directions,
reviewed and revised the arguments, and takes responsibility for the claims
in this paper.
