\section{Revealing-optimization instance optimality}
\label{sec:common-upper-instance}

Fix $u<\infty$.  An untouched job may be run raw for the public time $u$, or
tested for one unit; a test reveals $p_i\in[0,u]$, after which the processing
operation of length $p_i$ may be delayed.  The goal of this section is to
prove \cref{thm:common-upper-instance}, the rigorous version of the informal
revealing-optimization theorem \cref{thm:intro-common-upper-instance}.

For a distribution $D$ on $[0,u]$, we define an explicit value
$\PhiRO(D)$ in \cref{eq:cui-Phi}.  It is obtained by choosing one threshold
and minimizing a quadratic over the tested fraction $q$.  For an input
vector $p$, use the empirical distribution $D[p]$ from
\cref{eq:empirical-distributions}.

\begin{theorem}[Revealing-optimization instance optimality]
\label{thm:common-upper-instance}
For every fixed $u>0$ there exist randomized nonanticipating algorithms
$\mathcal A^*_{n,u}$, depending only on $n,u$, and a deterministic sequence
$\eps_u(n)\to0$ such that every vector $p\in[0,u]^n$ has the following
properties.
\begin{equation}
 \EE\ALG_{\mathcal A^*_{n,u}}(p)
 \le n^2\PhiRO(D[p])+\eps_u(n)n^2.
 \label{eq:cui-upper}
\end{equation}
Conversely, every $p\in[0,u]^n$ and every randomized nonanticipating algorithm
$\mathcal A$, even an announced algorithm, satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \EE\ALG_{\mathcal A}(p)
 \ge n^2\PhiRO(D[p])-\eps_u(n)n^2.
 \label{eq:cui-lower}
\end{equation}
One may take
\begin{equation}
 \eps_u(n)=O_u\!\left(n^{-1/6}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)}\right).
 \label{eq:cui-rate}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The proof has three steps.  First, for a fixed final tested fraction $q$, we
represent the work completed by a large population of jobs as divisible
blocks and order these blocks by work per completed job.  Second, we expose
the hidden values in the order in which the algorithm first touches their
labels.  A single sampling-without-replacement event controls every prefix of
this sequence, including a prefix whose endpoint the algorithm chooses from
its observations.  Finally, we discretize $[0,u]$ into increasingly many
cells and use a sublinear initial sample to choose the schedule without being
told the input vector.

\subsection{The fixed-fraction fluid benchmark}
\label{sec:cui-fluid}

Let $D$ initially have finite support and put
$\mu=\int p\,dD(p)$.
A test automatically completes a zero outcome.  Apply
\cref{lem:maximum-density-module} and choose its threshold selector $g_*$.
Write
\begin{equation}
 a=a_D(g_*),\qquad
 \ell=\int_{(0,u]}p g_*(p)\,dD(p),
 \qquad \tau_D=\frac{1+\ell}{a}.
 \label{eq:cui-tau}
\end{equation}
Write the positive support as $p_1,\ldots,p_s$, and let
$\nu_i=\nu(\{p_i\})$ be the residual masses from
\cref{lem:maximum-density-module}.  Thus
\begin{equation}
 \sum_i\nu_i=1-a,\qquad p_i\ge\tau_D\text{ whenever }\nu_i>0.
 \label{eq:cui-residual}
\end{equation}
For a fixed final tested fraction $q\in[0,1]$, define the model envelope
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_{\mathsf{RO},D,q}\defeq\mathcal C^u_{D,q},
 \label{eq:cui-model-envelope}
\end{equation}
where the right-hand side is the common envelope
\cref{eq:common-fluid-envelope} with independent-mode work $v=u$; the
independent mode is raw execution.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-$q$ completion envelope]
\label{lem:cui-envelope}
The envelope $\mathcal C_{\mathsf{RO},D,q}$ is the fractional completion
curve obtained from the following divisible blocks:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
item & completion capacity & work per completion\\
\midrule
maximum-density test module & $aq$ & $\tau_D$\\
residual tested class $i$ & $q\nu_i$ & $p_i$\\
raw module & $1-q$ & $u$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
If $\tau_D<u$, their optimal order is the test module, the residual classes in
nondecreasing $p_i$, and the raw module.  If $\tau_D\ge u$, the all-raw curve
pointwise dominates every choice with $q>0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem:test-module-envelope} with $v=u$.  It gives exactly the three
displayed kinds of items.  If $\tau_D<u$, their work-per-completion values obey
$\tau_D\le p_i\le u$.  If $\tau_D\ge u$, every test and residual item costs at
least $u$ per completion, while the $q=0$ envelope consists of one raw item
of capacity one and cost $u$.
\end{proof}

Put
\begin{equation}
 \ell_\nu=\sum_i p_i\nu_i=\mu-\ell,\qquad
 \SPT(\nu)=\frac12\sum_{i,j}\min\{p_i,p_j\}\nu_i\nu_j.
 \label{eq:cui-residual-moments}
\end{equation}
The common area identity \cref{eq:common-envelope-area}, specialized through
\cref{lem:cui-envelope}, gives
\begin{equation}
 F_{\mathsf{RO},u,D}(q)
 =(1+\ell)\left(q-\frac{aq^2}{2}\right)
 +q\ell_\nu(1-q)+q^2\SPT(\nu)
 +\frac{u(1-q)^2}{2}.
 \label{eq:cui-F}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the four terms are respectively the test-module contribution, the
work of positive tested jobs delaying raw jobs, the pair contribution within
the positive tested jobs, and the contribution of the equal-length raw jobs.
Define
\begin{equation}
 \PhiRO(D)=
 \begin{cases}
  \displaystyle\min_{0\le q\le1}F_{\mathsf{RO},u,D}(q),&\tau_D<u,\\[3pt]
  u/2,&\tau_D\ge u.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:cui-Phi}
\end{equation}
This is an explicit one-dimensional optimization.  In fact
\begin{equation}
 F_{\mathsf{RO},u,D}(q)=\frac u2+Aq+Bq^2,\qquad
 A=1+\mu-u,\qquad
 B=-\frac{a(1+\ell)}2-\ell_\nu+\SPT(\nu)+\frac u2.
 \label{eq:cui-quadratic}
\end{equation}
For each work budget, convexly combining feasible points for $q_1,q_2$
gives a feasible point for the corresponding convex combination of the two
fractions.  Hence $\mathcal C_{\mathsf{RO},D,q}(x)$ is concave in $q$, its remaining-mass area is
convex, and $B\ge0$.  Moreover,
\[
 \tau_D<u\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\int(u-p)\,dD(p)>1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A<0.
\]
Thus the minimizer is
\begin{equation}
 q^*=1\quad(B=0),\qquad
 q^*=\min\{1,-A/(2B)\}\quad(B>0).
 \label{eq:cui-qstar}
\end{equation}
Thus, for every divisible schedule whose final tested fraction is $q$, the
area under its remaining-job curve is at least
$F_{\mathsf{RO},u,D}(q)\ge\PhiRO(D)$.

\subsection{Adaptive algorithms and the announced lower bound}
\label{sec:cui-announced}

We now transfer the divisible-job bound to a finite algorithm which may choose
labels and decide when to stop testing from the values seen so far.  Treat
equal-valued jobs as distinct jobs, and first fix the private random seed of an
algorithm that eventually completes every job.  For the present
finite-support distribution, let $C_0=\{0\}$ and
$C_j=\{p_j\}$ for $1\le j\le K=s$, and put $d_j=D(C_j)$.
Apply \cref{lem:uniform-first-touch-event} with failure probability $n^{-2}$,
using its notation $X_k,A_k$, and let $\Gamma_n=\Gamma_{\rm cell}$.  With
that probability, simultaneously
for every cell $j$ and every touch prefix $m\le(1-\delta)n$,
\begin{equation}
 \left|\sum_{k\le m}A_k\one_{X_k\in C_j}
       -d_j\sum_{k\le m}A_k\right|\le\Gamma_n.
 \label{eq:cui-urn-discrepancy}
\end{equation}

Stop the first-touch sequence when only $\delta n+O(1)$ labels remain.  In
the suffix, replace every future test of an untouched job by raw execution
at its first original operation and delete its later known processing.
At most $\delta n+O(1)$ jobs and three operations per affected job change.
Thus \cref{lem:bounded-suffix-edit}, with $W=u+1$, gives
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm original}\ge C_{\rm edited}-O_u(\delta n^2+n).
 \label{eq:cui-suffix-edit}
\end{equation}
The edited schedule has a fixed final tested fraction $q$, but we do not
condition on its value.

On the single sampling event \cref{eq:cui-urn-discrepancy}, consider any
operation prefix and
normalize counts and work by $n$.  Let $t,b,z,c_j$ denote tested mass, raw
mass, tested-zero completions, and processed known mass from positive cell
$C_j$, and put $\gamma_n=\Gamma_n/n$.  The sampling event gives the capacity
hypotheses of \cref{lem:cell-capacity-repair}; raw work is exactly $ub$, so
its work hypothesis holds with $\alpha=0$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \text{completed fraction at work }x
 \le \mathcal C_{\mathsf{RO},D,q}(x)+(K+1)\gamma_n.
 \label{eq:cui-envelope-repair}
\end{equation}
The completion curve reaches one by normalized work at most $H=1+u$, and
$0\le\PhiRO(D)\le u/2$.  Thus \cref{thm:completion-curve-transfer}, with
the suffix edit as $\varepsilon=O_u(\delta+1/n)$ and
$\beta=(K+1)\gamma_n$, gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{C_{\rm original}}{n^2}
 \ge\PhiRO(D)-O_{u,K}(\gamma_n+\delta+1/n).
 \label{eq:cui-fixed-grid-lower}
\end{equation}

For an arbitrary input vector, take
\begin{equation}
 K_n=\lfloor n^{1/6}\rfloor,\qquad
 h_n=u/K_n,\qquad \delta_n=n^{-1/6},
 \label{eq:cui-grid-parameters}
\end{equation}
keep zero separate, and round every positive value upward.  If two
distributions admit a zero-preserving coupling with pointwise displacement
at most $h$, the transport lemma \cref{lem:zero-preserving-transport} gives
\begin{equation}
 |\PhiRO(D)-\PhiRO(D')|\le h.
 \label{eq:cui-rounding-stability}
\end{equation}
Thus the grid consists of $0,h_n,2h_n,\ldots,K_nh_n=u$, and a value in
$((j-1)h_n,jh_n]$ is rounded to $jh_n$.  For the finite repair, raising a
processed value to this right endpoint adds at
most $h_n$ to normalized prefix work, while
\cref{lem:cell-capacity-repair} loses at most
$(K_n+1)\gamma_n$ completed mass.  With the parameters above,
\begin{equation}
 (K_n+1)\frac{\Gamma_n}{n}
 =O\!\left(n^{-1/6}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)}\right).
 \label{eq:cui-grid-error}
\end{equation}
Apply \cref{thm:completion-curve-transfer} directly to the rounded grid, with
$\alpha=h_n$, $\beta=(K_n+1)\gamma_n$, $H=1+u$, and
$B=u/2$.  Together with the suffix edit and
\cref{eq:cui-rounding-stability,eq:cui-grid-error}, it proves that every
announced algorithm has private-shuffle cost at least
\begin{equation}
 n^2\PhiRO(D[p])
 -O_u\!\left(n^{11/6}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)}\right).
 \label{eq:cui-announced-lower}
\end{equation}
Condition on the algorithm seed when applying the transfer theorem and then
average over the seed.  This proves \cref{eq:cui-lower} under the input
convention.

\subsection{Removing the announcement}
\label{sec:cui-learning}

It remains to attain the benchmark without first revealing the input vector
to the algorithm.  Consider the following family of schedules.  Select
$N_{\rm test}$ labels uniformly, test them in random order, and immediately
process precisely the outcomes belonging to a fixed initial segment of the
ordered grid values.  Next process the remaining positive tested jobs in SPT
order and finally run the other $n-N_{\rm test}$ jobs raw.  Call the choice of
the initial grid segment and $q=N_{\rm test}/n$ a template $\pi$.  Direct
one-job and pair accounting gives, uniformly over the frequencies of the
grid values and the chosen initial segment,
\begin{equation}
 \EE C_n(\pi)=n^2F_{\mathsf{RO},u,D}(\pi)+O_u(n),
 \label{eq:cui-finite-kernel}
\end{equation}
where
$F_{\mathsf{RO},u,D}(\pi)$ is the right-hand side of \cref{eq:cui-F}.
To verify this, expand the testing phase into the contribution of each job
and each pair of tested jobs; expand the remaining positive tested block into
its contribution against raw jobs and its internal SPT cost; and add the
exact raw-block cost
$u(n-N_{\rm test})(n-N_{\rm test}+1)/2$.  For distinct occurrences the finite
coefficients
\[
 \frac{N_{\rm test}(N_{\rm test}-1)}{n(n-1)},\qquad
 \frac{N_{\rm test}(n-N_{\rm test})}{n(n-1)},\qquad
 \frac{(n-N_{\rm test})(n-N_{\rm test}-1)}{n(n-1)}
\]
differ from $q^2,q(1-q),(1-q)^2$ by $O(1/n)$.  Every pair contribution is
bounded in terms of $u$, so fewer than $n^2$ such terms create only
$O_u(n)$ error.

Use the grid from \cref{eq:cui-grid-parameters} and take a private random
permutation.  Test its first
\begin{equation}
 k_n=\lceil n^{1/2}\rceil
 \label{eq:cui-pilot-size}
\end{equation}
labels, keep positive sample jobs pending, and form their rounded histogram
$\widehat D_n$.  Among all grid prefixes containing zero and all
$q\in[0,1]$, minimize the quadratic \cref{eq:cui-F}.  Apply the selected
template to the remaining labels in their unused random order, then finish
the positive sample jobs.

Let $D_n$ and $D_n^{\rm rem}$ be the full and remaining rounded histograms.
The sampling-without-replacement histogram estimate
\cref{eq:without-replacement-histogram} gives
\begin{equation}
 \EE\|\widehat D_n-D_n\|_1
 \le\sqrt{\frac{K_n+1}{k_n}},
 \qquad
 \|D_n^{\rm rem}-D_n\|_1\le\frac{2k_n}{n-k_n}.
 \label{eq:cui-histogram}
\end{equation}
For a fixed template, the quantities $a,\ell,\ell_\nu$ in
\cref{eq:cui-F} are bounded single integrals and $\SPT(\nu)$ is a bounded
double integral.  In the latter, add and subtract the same double integral
with only one copy of the distribution replaced.  This gives
\begin{equation}
 |F_{\mathsf{RO},u,D}(\pi)-F_{\mathsf{RO},u,E}(\pi)|
 \le4(u+1)\|D-E\|_1,
 \label{eq:cui-template-Lipschitz}
\end{equation}
uniformly over the grid and template.  The same bound holds for the minima.
The maximum-density and completion-envelope results
\cref{lem:maximum-density-module,lem:cui-envelope} show that the minimum
over this common template family is exactly $\PhiRO(D)$.

Condition on the sample.  The learned template is fixed and the unused labels
are uniformly ordered, so \cref{eq:cui-finite-kernel} applies.  Including the
sample and its final tail, whose cost is $O_u(nk_n)$, gives the conditional
hypothesis of \cref{lem:pilot-learning-transfer} with
$\varepsilon=O_u(k_n/n+1/n)$.  Apply that lemma with
$d(D,E)=\|D-E\|_1$, $L_0=4(u+1)$, and \cref{eq:cui-histogram}.  Finally use
\cref{eq:cui-rounding-stability}.  Since
\[
 h_n=O_u(n^{-1/6}),\qquad
 \sqrt{(K_n+1)/k_n}=O(n^{-1/6}),\qquad
 k_n/n=O(n^{-1/2}),
\]
the resulting bound is
\begin{equation}
 \EE\ALG_{\mathcal A^*_{n,u}}(p)
 \le n^2\PhiRO(D[p])+O_u(n^{11/6})
 \label{eq:cui-universal-upper}
\end{equation}
for every $p\in[0,u]^n$.  Together with the announced lower bound
\cref{eq:cui-announced-lower}, this proves
\cref{thm:common-upper-instance}.  The larger logarithmic lower-bound error
gives the common sequence in \cref{eq:cui-rate}.
