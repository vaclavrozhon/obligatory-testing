\section{Revealing-optimization instance optimality}
\label{sec:common-upper-instance}

Fix $u<\infty$.  An untouched job may be run raw for the public time $u$, or
tested for one unit; a test reveals $p_i\in[0,u]$, after which the processing
operation of length $p_i$ may be delayed.  The goal of this section is to
prove \cref{thm:common-upper-instance}, the rigorous version of the informal
revealing-optimization theorem \cref{thm:intro-common-upper-instance}.

For a distribution $D$ on $[0,u]$, we define an explicit value
$\Phi_u(D)$ in \cref{eq:cui-Phi}.  It is obtained by choosing one threshold
and minimizing a quadratic over the tested fraction $q$.  For a multiset
$M$, write $D_M$ for its empirical distribution.

\begin{theorem}[Revealing-optimization instance optimality]
\label{thm:common-upper-instance}
For every fixed $u>0$ there exist randomized nonanticipating policies
$\mathcal A^*_{n,u}$, depending only on $n,u$, and a deterministic sequence
$\eps_u(n)\to0$ such that every size-$n$ multiset
$M\subseteq[0,u]$ has the following properties.
For every fixed labeling $\sigma$,
\begin{equation}
 \EE\cost(\mathcal A^*_{n,u},\sigma(M))
 \le n^2\Phi_u(D_M)+\eps_u(n)n^2.
 \label{eq:cui-upper}
\end{equation}
Conversely, for every randomized nonanticipating policy $\mathcal A$, even
one told $M$, some fixed oblivious labeling $\sigma$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \EE\cost(\mathcal A,\sigma(M))
 \ge n^2\Phi_u(D_M)-\eps_u(n)n^2.
 \label{eq:cui-lower}
\end{equation}
One may take
\begin{equation}
 \eps_u(n)=O_u\!\left(n^{-1/6}\sqrt{\log(n+2)}\right).
 \label{eq:cui-rate}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

The proof has three layers.  First, for a fixed final tested fraction $q$, a
completion envelope reduces every fluid schedule to an ordinary fractional
knapsack.  Second, an adaptive first-touch trace turns every deterministic
policy under a uniform placement into predictable sampling without
replacement.  One event then controls every touch prefix, so the realized
stopping fraction can be substituted pathwise.  Finally, a growing grid and
a sublinear sample make the result uniform over arbitrary bounded multisets
without announcing the input.  The first subsection begins the formal proof.

\subsection{The fixed-fraction fluid benchmark}
\label{sec:cui-fluid}

Let $D$ initially have finite support, let $P\sim D$, and put
\[
 D_0=\Pr(P=0),\qquad \mu=\EE P.
\]
A test automatically completes a zero outcome.  For a rule
$0\le g\le1$ selecting positive outcomes to process immediately, define
\begin{equation}
 A(g)=D_0+\EE[g(P)\one_{P>0}],
 \qquad
 W(g)=1+\EE[P g(P)\one_{P>0}].
 \label{eq:cui-module-density}
\end{equation}
Choose $g^*$ maximizing $A(g)/W(g)$, put
\begin{equation}
 a=A(g^*),\qquad \ell=\EE[P g^*(P)\one_{P>0}],
 \qquad \tau=\frac{1+\ell}{a},
 \label{eq:cui-tau}
\end{equation}
and interpret the ratio as zero when its numerator vanishes.

\begin{lemma}[Maximum-density threshold]
\label{lem:cui-threshold}
There is a maximizer which selects every $p<\tau$, rejects every
$p>\tau$, and may split an atom at equality.  It satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \EE(\tau-P)^+=1,
 \qquad
 \tau\bigl(D_0+\EE[g(P)\one_{P>0}]\bigr)
 \le 1+\EE[P g(P)\one_{P>0}]
 \label{eq:cui-density-dual}
\end{equation}
for every $0\le g\le1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If selected mass $d$ at a positive value $p$ is added to a candidate with
density $\rho$, cross multiplication says that the density increases exactly
when $1/p>\rho$.  Removing mass gives the reverse implication.  A maximizer
may therefore be chosen as a threshold with boundary $1/\rho=\tau$.
The identity $1+\ell=a\tau$ expands to
\[
 1=\tau D_0+\EE[(\tau-P)g^*(P)\one_{P>0}]
  =\EE(\tau-P)^+.
\]
Maximality gives $A(g)/W(g)\le1/\tau$ for every $g$; clearing the positive
denominators proves the second inequality in
\cref{eq:cui-density-dual}.
\end{proof}

Split a boundary atom according to $g^*$.  Let $R_i$ be the residual mass at
positive support value $p_i$.  Thus
\begin{equation}
 \sum_iR_i=1-a,\qquad p_i\ge\tau\text{ whenever }R_i>0.
 \label{eq:cui-residual}
\end{equation}
For a fixed final tested fraction $q\in[0,1]$, consider any prefix of a fluid
schedule and write $t$ for tested mass, $b$ for raw-completed mass, and
$c_i$ for processed known mass of class $i$.  Its completed mass and work
are
\begin{equation}
 y=D_0t+b+\sum_i c_i,\qquad
 x=t+ub+\sum_i p_ic_i,
 \label{eq:cui-prefix}
\end{equation}
and every feasible prefix satisfies
\begin{equation}
 0\le t\le q,\qquad 0\le b\le1-q,\qquad
 0\le c_i\le D_it.
 \label{eq:cui-revelation}
\end{equation}
Let $H^u_{D,q}(x)$ be the maximum completed mass subject to
\cref{eq:cui-prefix,eq:cui-revelation} and work at most $x$.

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-$q$ completion envelope]
\label{lem:cui-envelope}
The envelope $H^u_{D,q}$ is the divisible-knapsack curve with items
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
\toprule
item & completion capacity & work per completion\\
\midrule
maximum-density test module & $aq$ & $\tau$\\
residual tested class $i$ & $qR_i$ & $p_i$\\
raw module & $1-q$ & $u$\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
If $\tau<u$, the greedy order is the test module, the residual classes in
nondecreasing $p_i$, and the raw module.  If $\tau\ge u$, the all-raw curve
pointwise dominates every choice with $q>0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a feasible prefix, let $y_L,x_L$ be the completion and work contributed
by the tests, tested zeros, and processed selected positive outcomes.
Apply \cref{eq:cui-density-dual} to the processed fractions within the first
$t$ units of tests.  It gives
\[
 \tau y_L\le x_L,\qquad y_L\le at.
\]
With $\lambda=y_L/a$, replacing this part by $\lambda$ full test modules
preserves completed mass, weakly decreases work, and uses
$0\le\lambda\le t\le q$.  Residual class $i$ has capacity at most $qR_i$,
and the raw mass has capacity at most $1-q$.  Thus every feasible prefix maps
to the displayed knapsack.

Conversely, while greedy is partway through the test-module item, run the
corresponding fraction of tests and process their selected outcomes.  Once
all $q$ tests have run, every residual capacity is available; the other
items can then be processed in greedy order.  A partial final item is
realized fractionally.  The knapsack curve is therefore the closure of the
original fluid trajectories.  Fractional knapsack orders items by increasing
work per completion.  In the short-test regime,
$\tau\le p_i\le u$, which gives the claimed order.  In the other regime all
test and residual items cost at least $u$, while one raw item of capacity one
costs exactly $u$.
\end{proof}

Put
\begin{equation}
 m_R=\sum_i p_iR_i=\mu-\ell,\qquad
 K_R=\sum_{i,j}\min\{p_i,p_j\}R_iR_j.
 \label{eq:cui-residual-moments}
\end{equation}
Integrating the envelope of \cref{lem:cui-envelope} gives
\begin{equation}
 F_{u,D}(q)
 =(1+\ell)\left(q-\frac{aq^2}{2}\right)
 +qm_R(1-q)+\frac{q^2K_R}{2}
 +\frac{u(1-q)^2}{2}.
 \label{eq:cui-F}
\end{equation}
Indeed, the four terms are respectively the test-module area, residual work
delaying the raw block, internal residual SPT pairs, and the homogeneous raw
block.  Define
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_u(D)=
 \begin{cases}
  \displaystyle\min_{0\le q\le1}F_{u,D}(q),&\tau<u,\\[3pt]
  u/2,&\tau\ge u.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:cui-Phi}
\end{equation}
This is an explicit one-dimensional optimization.  In fact
\begin{equation}
 F_{u,D}(q)=\frac u2+Aq+Bq^2,\qquad
 A=1+\mu-u,\qquad
 B=-\frac{a(1+\ell)}2-m_R+\frac{K_R}2+\frac u2.
 \label{eq:cui-quadratic}
\end{equation}
For each work budget, convexly combining feasible points for $q_1,q_2$
gives a feasible point for the corresponding convex combination of the two
fractions.  Hence $H^u_{D,q}(x)$ is concave in $q$, its remaining-mass area is
convex, and $B\ge0$.  Moreover,
\[
 \tau<u\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\EE(u-P)>1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad A<0.
\]
Thus the minimizer is
\begin{equation}
 q^*=1\quad(B=0),\qquad
 q^*=\min\{1,-A/(2B)\}\quad(B>0).
 \label{eq:cui-qstar}
\end{equation}
We have proved pathwise at the fluid level that a policy with realized final
tested fraction $q$ has area at least
$F_{u,D}(q)\ge\Phi_u(D)$.

\subsection{Adaptive policies and the announced lower bound}
\label{sec:cui-announced}

We now transfer the fluid envelope to a finite policy which may choose labels
and its stopping time adaptively.  Equal-valued jobs are first distinguished
as occurrence tokens.  Let $J$ be the public labels, $\Omega$ the tokens,
$v:\Omega\to[0,u]$ their values, and let a placement
$\sigma:J\to\Omega$ be a bijection.  Fix the private seed of a completing
deterministic policy.  In its run on $\sigma$, list the labels in order of
first touch,
\[
 \ell_1(\sigma),\ldots,\ell_n(\sigma),
\]
where a first touch is a test or a raw execution, and define
\begin{equation}
 R_{\mathcal A}(\sigma)_k=\sigma(\ell_k(\sigma)).
 \label{eq:cui-reveal-order}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Adaptive first-touch bijection]
\label{lem:cui-trace-bijection}
The map $\sigma\mapsto R_{\mathcal A}(\sigma)$ is a bijection from placements
to permutations of $\Omega$.  Whether touch $k$ is a test or a raw execution
is determined by the preceding hidden values
$v(R_{\mathcal A}(\sigma)_1),\ldots,
v(R_{\mathcal A}(\sigma)_{k-1})$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Suppose two placements have the same occurrence order.  A lockstep induction
shows that the two runs have the same observable history before every first
touch.  Between touches, equal histories prescribe the same known-job
operations.  At the next touch they prescribe the same fresh label and the
same test/raw choice.  A test then reveals the same next token value; a raw
operation lasts the same public time $u$ and reveals no value.  Thus the
first-touch labels agree at every position.  Equality of the occurrence
orders now gives equality of the placements on a permutation of all labels.
The map is injective between finite sets of the same cardinality $n!$, hence
bijective.  The same induction stopped after a common value prefix proves
predictability.  The hidden value of a raw occurrence is extra analytical
information and therefore cannot invalidate that conclusion.
\end{proof}

Under a uniform placement, the occurrence order is consequently a uniform
permutation.  Partition $[0,u]$ into $K+1$ cells, keeping zero separate, and
let $D_j$ be the exact mass of cell $j$.  If $X_k$ is the value at touch $k$
and $A_k$ is the predictable indicator that this touch is a test, the
following event is uniform over the policy.

\begin{lemma}[Predictable urn discrepancy]
\label{lem:cui-urn}
Fix $\delta\in(0,1)$ and put
\begin{equation}
 e_n=\sqrt{8n\log(n(K+2))},
 \qquad
 \Gamma_n=(1+\delta^{-1})e_n.
 \label{eq:cui-Gamma}
\end{equation}
With probability $1-o(1)$, simultaneously for every cell $j$ and every touch
prefix $m\le(1-\delta)n$,
\begin{equation}
 \left|\sum_{k\le m}A_k\one_{X_k\in j}
       -D_j\sum_{k\le m}A_k\right|\le\Gamma_n.
 \label{eq:cui-urn-discrepancy}
\end{equation}
If $K$ is polynomial in $n$, the failure probability is inverse polynomial.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The uniform-prefix sampling bound
\cref{eq:uniform-prefix-hoeffding}, applied to all cells, gives
$|N_j(k)-kD_j|\le e_n$ simultaneously, except with inverse-polynomial
probability.  While $k\le(1-\delta)n$, the remaining proportion
$R_{k,j}$ therefore obeys
$|R_{k,j}-D_j|\le e_n/(\delta n)$.  Since $A_k$ is predictable,
\[
 \sum_{k\le m}A_k(\one_{X_k\in j}-R_{k-1,j})
\]
is a martingale with increments bounded by one.  The maximal
Azuma--Hoeffding bound \cref{eq:maximal-azuma}, again union-bounded over the cells, places its running
maximum below $e_n$.  Adding the predictable drift gives
\cref{eq:cui-urn-discrepancy}.
\end{proof}

Stop the first-touch sequence when only $\delta n+O(1)$ labels remain.  In
the suffix, replace every future test of an untouched job by raw execution
at its first original operation and delete its later known processing.
Only $O(\delta n)$ bounded operations change, so
\begin{equation}
 C_{\rm original}\ge C_{\rm edited}-O_u(\delta n^2+n).
 \label{eq:cui-suffix-edit}
\end{equation}
The edited schedule has a fixed final tested fraction $q$, but we do not
condition on its value.

On the single event of \cref{lem:cui-urn}, consider any operation prefix.
Normalize counts and work by $n$; let $t,b,z,c_j$ denote tested mass, raw
mass, tested-zero completions, and processed known mass from positive cell
$j$.  With $\gamma_n=\Gamma_n/n$,
\[
 z\le D_0t+\gamma_n,\qquad c_j\le D_jt+\gamma_n.
\]
Set $c_j'=(c_j-\gamma_n)^+$.  Then
\begin{equation}
 c_j'\le D_jt,\qquad t\le q,\qquad b\le1-q.
 \label{eq:cui-repaired-capacities}
\end{equation}
Raw work is exactly $ub$, and reducing known mass reduces work.  Thus the
repaired point is feasible for the empirical envelope at the actual work
budget, while completed mass decreases by at most $(K+1)\gamma_n$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \text{completed fraction at work }x
 \le H^u_{D,q}(x)+(K+1)\gamma_n.
 \label{eq:cui-envelope-repair}
\end{equation}
Integrating the remaining-mass curve, substituting the realized $q$ only
after \cref{eq:cui-envelope-repair} is established, and restoring
\cref{eq:cui-suffix-edit} gives the fixed-grid lower bound
\begin{equation}
 \frac{C_{\rm original}}{n^2}
 \ge\Phi_u(D)-O_{u,K}(\gamma_n+\delta+1/n).
 \label{eq:cui-fixed-grid-lower}
\end{equation}

For an arbitrary multiset, take
\begin{equation}
 K_n=\lfloor n^{1/6}\rfloor,\qquad
 h_n=u/K_n,\qquad \delta_n=n^{-1/6},
 \label{eq:cui-grid-parameters}
\end{equation}
keep zero separate, and round every positive value upward.  If two
distributions admit a zero-preserving coupling with pointwise displacement
at most $h$, transporting the same fluid policy changes total known
processing work by at most $h$ and hence changes its remaining-mass area by
at most $h$.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 |\Phi_u(D)-\Phi_u(D')|\le h.
 \label{eq:cui-rounding-stability}
\end{equation}
For the finite repair, raising a processed value to its bin endpoint shifts
the envelope horizontally by at most $h_n$; deleting $\gamma_n$ from every
cell creates vertical error $(K_n+1)\gamma_n$.  With the parameters above,
\begin{equation}
 (K_n+1)\frac{\Gamma_n}{n}
 =O\!\left(n^{-1/6}\sqrt{\log(n+2)}\right).
 \label{eq:cui-grid-error}
\end{equation}
On the bad event we use nonnegativity and $0\le\Phi_u\le u/2$.
Averaging \cref{eq:cui-fixed-grid-lower}, using
\cref{eq:cui-rounding-stability,eq:cui-grid-error}, and then averaging over
the private seed proves that every announced policy has uniform-placement
cost at least
\begin{equation}
 n^2\Phi_u(D_M)
 -O_u\!\left(n^{11/6}\sqrt{\log(n+2)}\right).
 \label{eq:cui-announced-lower}
\end{equation}
At least one placement has seed-expected cost at least this average.  It is
chosen before the seed and is therefore a fixed oblivious labeling.  This
proves \cref{eq:cui-lower}.

\subsection{A universal policy without the multiset}
\label{sec:cui-learning}

It remains to attain the benchmark without being told $M$.  We first record
the finite cost of one canonical template: select $r$ labels uniformly,
test them in random order while immediately processing outcomes in a fixed
low prefix, then process the tested residual stock in SPT order and finally
run the other $n-r$ jobs raw.  If $q=r/n$, direct pair accounting gives,
uniformly over the empirical grid histogram and the chosen prefix,
\begin{equation}
 \EE C_n(\pi)=n^2\Psi_D(\pi)+O_u(n),
 \label{eq:cui-finite-kernel}
\end{equation}
where $\Psi_D(\pi)$ is the right-hand side of
\cref{eq:cui-F}.  To verify this, expand the testing phase into one-job terms
and relative-order kernels for pairs of tested jobs; expand the residual SPT
block into its work against raw jobs and its minimum kernel; and add the
exact raw-block cost $u(n-r)(n-r+1)/2$.  For distinct occurrences the finite
coefficients
\[
 \frac{r(r-1)}{n(n-1)},\qquad
 \frac{r(n-r)}{n(n-1)},\qquad
 \frac{(n-r)(n-r-1)}{n(n-1)}
\]
differ from $q^2,q(1-q),(1-q)^2$ by $O(1/n)$.  Every kernel is bounded in
terms of $u$, so fewer than $n^2$ pair terms create only $O_u(n)$ error.

Use the grid from \cref{eq:cui-grid-parameters} and take a private random
permutation.  Test its first
\begin{equation}
 k_n=\lceil n^{1/2}\rceil
 \label{eq:cui-pilot-size}
\end{equation}
labels, keep positive sample jobs pending, and form their rounded histogram
$\widehat D_n$.  Among all grid prefixes containing zero and all
$q\in[0,1]$, minimize the quadratic \cref{eq:cui-F}.  Apply the selected
template to the remaining labels in their unused random order, then finish
the positive sample jobs.

Let $D_n$ and $D_n^R$ be the full and remaining rounded histograms.
The sampling-without-replacement histogram estimate
\cref{eq:without-replacement-histogram} gives
\begin{equation}
 \EE\|\widehat D_n-D_n\|_1
 \le\sqrt{\frac{K_n+1}{k_n}},
 \qquad
 \|D_n^R-D_n\|_1\le\frac{2k_n}{n-k_n}.
 \label{eq:cui-histogram}
\end{equation}
For a fixed template, the quantities $a,\ell,m_R$ in
\cref{eq:cui-F} are bounded one-draw expectations and $K_R$ is a bounded
two-draw expectation.  Inserting an intermediate product measure shows
\begin{equation}
 |\Psi_D(\pi)-\Psi_E(\pi)|
 \le4(u+1)\|D-E\|_1,
 \label{eq:cui-template-Lipschitz}
\end{equation}
uniformly over the grid and template.  The same bound holds for the minima.
The threshold lemma and completion envelope show that the minimum over this
common template family is exactly $\Phi_u(D)$.

Condition on the sample.  The learned template is fixed and the unused labels
are uniformly ordered, so \cref{eq:cui-finite-kernel} applies.  The usual
empirical-minimization sandwich, twice using
\cref{eq:cui-template-Lipschitz}, bounds the main cost by
\[
 (n-k_n)^2\left(
 \Phi_u(D_n^R)+O_u(\|\widehat D_n-D_n^R\|_1)\right)+O_u(n).
\]
The sample and its final tail contribute $O_u(nk_n)$.  Now apply
\cref{eq:cui-histogram}, replace the remaining histogram by the full one,
and use \cref{eq:cui-rounding-stability}.  Since
\[
 h_n=O_u(n^{-1/6}),\qquad
 \sqrt{(K_n+1)/k_n}=O(n^{-1/6}),\qquad
 k_n/n=O(n^{-1/2}),
\]
the resulting bound is
\begin{equation}
 \EE\cost(\mathcal A^*_{n,u},\sigma(M))
 \le n^2\Phi_u(D_M)+O_u(n^{11/6})
 \label{eq:cui-universal-upper}
\end{equation}
for every fixed labeling.  Together with the announced lower bound
\cref{eq:cui-announced-lower}, this proves
\cref{thm:common-upper-instance}.  The larger logarithmic lower-bound error
gives the common sequence in \cref{eq:cui-rate}.
