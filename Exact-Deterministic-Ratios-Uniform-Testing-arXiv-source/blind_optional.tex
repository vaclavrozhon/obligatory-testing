\section{Blind execution: instance-optimal four-block policies}
\label{sec:blind}

We now turn to $\mathsf{BE}(L)$.  Running an untouched job blindly consumes
its actual processing time and completes it; testing consumes one unit,
reveals the value, and permits the processing operation to be delayed.  The
adversary fixes an arbitrary labeled vector in $[0,L]^n$ before the private
seed is drawn.  The goal of this section is to prove
\cref{thm:blind-instance}, the rigorous version of the informal
blind-execution theorem \cref{thm:intro-blind-instance}.

\begin{theorem}[Blind-execution instance optimality]
\label{thm:blind-instance}
Fix $L<\infty$.  There exist a randomized nonanticipating policy $\mathcal A^*$,
which knows $n,L$ but not the processing-time multiset, and a deterministic
sequence $\eps_L(n)\to0$ with the following properties.  For every multiset
$M\subseteq[0,L]$ of size $n$ and every fixed labeling $\sigma$,
\begin{equation}
 \EE\cost(\mathcal A^*,\sigma(M))
 \le n^2\PhiBE(D[M])+\eps_L(n)n^2.
 \label{eq:intro-blind-upper}
\end{equation}
For every randomized policy $\mathcal A$, even an announced policy, some
fixed oblivious labeling $\sigma$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \EE\cost(\mathcal A,\sigma(M))
 \ge n^2\PhiBE(D[M])-\eps_L(n)n^2.
 \label{eq:intro-blind-lower}
\end{equation}
The benchmark $\PhiBE$ is defined explicitly in
\cref{sec:blind-benchmark}; it is the minimum of a one-variable quadratic.
One may take
$\eps_L(n)=O_L(n^{-1/6}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)})$.
\end{theorem}

The boundedness assumption in \cref{thm:blind-instance} cannot be removed.
In fact, without it even the much weaker worst-case guarantee is impossible.

\begin{theorem}[No finite ratio for unbounded blind execution]
\label{thm:blind-unbounded-impossible}
There are no randomized policies $\mathcal A_n$, constant $R<\infty$, and
sequence $\eta(n)\to0$ such that every vector
$p\in[0,\infty)^n$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \EE\cost(\mathcal A_n,p)
 \le R\cost(\OPT,p)+\eta(n)n^2.
 \label{eq:blind-unbounded-impossible}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that such a guarantee exists, and run $\mathcal A_n$ on the all-zero
instance.  Its offline cost is zero, so if $Z_n$ denotes the expected online
cost, then $Z_n\le\eta(n)n^2=o(n^2)$.  We may assume that the policy finishes
every job, since otherwise its cost is infinite.

Fix its private seed and order the labels by their first touch on this zero
instance.  Every first touch completes its job: a blind zero takes no time,
while a tested zero completes with its unit test.  If the job at rank $h$ is
tested, that test is performed while $n-h+1$ jobs are unfinished and hence
contributes $n-h+1$ to the objective.  Consequently
\begin{equation}
 Z_n\ge\EE\sum_{h:\,\text{rank }h\text{ is tested}}(n-h+1).
 \label{eq:blind-zero-test-area}
\end{equation}

Now choose one label uniformly and change only its length from zero to
$H=n^2$.  Until this exceptional label is first touched, the policy sees
exactly the zero-instance history.  If its rank $h$ is touched blindly, its
nonpreemptive execution occupies the machine for $H$ time units while
$n-h+1$ jobs are unfinished.  Averaging over the exceptional label and the
private seed therefore gives
\begin{align}
 \EE\cost(\mathcal A_n,p)
 &\ge \frac Hn\,\EE
   \sum_{h:\,\text{rank }h\text{ is blind}}(n-h+1)\notag\\
 &\ge\frac Hn\left(\frac{n(n+1)}2-Z_n\right)
  =H\left(\frac n2-o(n)\right).
 \label{eq:blind-one-long-average}
\end{align}
Some fixed choice of the exceptional label has at least this expected cost.
For every such labeling, the clairvoyant schedule runs all zero jobs first
and the long job last, so $\cost(\OPT,p)=H$.  With $H=n^2$,
\cref{eq:blind-one-long-average} contradicts
\cref{eq:blind-unbounded-impossible} for all sufficiently large $n$.
\end{proof}

The proof first replaces a round of tests and its immediate short outcomes
by one divisible completion module.  For a fixed tested fraction $q$, a
fractional-knapsack envelope then forces the four-block order from the
introduction and gives its exact quadratic cost.  To handle an arbitrary
adaptive policy, we show that its first-touch order is a bijective
relabeling of the occurrence permutation; predictable sampling controls all
touch prefixes at once.  A growing grid extends the bound to arbitrary
bounded multisets, and a blind sample removes advance knowledge of the input.

\subsection{The distributional benchmark}
\label{sec:blind-benchmark}

Let $D$ be a probability distribution on $[0,L]$ and let $P\sim D$.  Write
\begin{equation}
 \mu=\EE P.
 \label{eq:blind-mean}
\end{equation}
Blind execution completes one unit of job mass using expected work $\mu$.
Apply \cref{lem:maximum-density-module} to $D$, choose its threshold selector
$g_*$, and let $\nu$ be the resulting residual measure.  The best possible
test-module density and its reciprocal are
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_D\defeq\max_g\frac{a_D(g)}{w_D(g)}=\frac1{\tau_D}.
 \label{eq:blind-density}
\end{equation}
The selector processes all positive values below $\tau_D$ immediately after
their tests, rejects all values above it, and may split a boundary atom.  With
that boundary convention, put
\[
 a=a_D(g_*),\qquad \ell=\EE[P g_*(P)\one_{P>0}].
\]
The identities in \cref{lem:maximum-density-module} give
\begin{equation}
 a\tau_D-\ell=1,
 \qquad \tau_D=\frac{1+\ell}{a}.
 \label{eq:blind-test-module}
\end{equation}
Thus a full unit of tests together with all selected low outcomes is a
divisible module of completion mass $a$, work $1+\ell$, and work per
completion $\tau_D$.

If $\tau_D\ge\mu$, blind work has at least the completion density of any test
module, and pure blind execution is optimal at the fluid scale.  Suppose now
that $\tau_D<\mu$.  After testing a fraction $q$, the canonical schedule is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \underbrace{\text{tests with immediate }p<\tau_D}_{\text{discovery}}
 &\ \longrightarrow\
 \underbrace{\text{known }\tau_D<p<\mu\text{ in SPT}}_{\text{medium}}\\[-2pt]
 &\ \longrightarrow\
 \underbrace{\text{untouched jobs blind}}_{\text{blind block}}
 \ \longrightarrow\
 \underbrace{\text{known }p>\mu\text{ in SPT}}_{\text{high tail}}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:blind-four-block-order}
\end{equation}

For the explicit cost, first assume no atom lies at $\tau_D$ or $\mu$, and put
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal L&=\{p<\tau_D\},&
 \mathcal M&=\{\tau_D<p<\mu\},&
 \mathcal H&=\{p>\mu\},\\
 a&=\Prob(P\in\mathcal L),&
 \ell&=\EE[P\one_{\mathcal L}],&
 \ell_M&=\EE[P\one_{\mathcal M}],\\
 d&=\Prob(P\in\mathcal H),&
 K_M&=\EE[\min(P,P')\one_{P,P'\in\mathcal M}],&
 K_H&=\EE[\min(P,P')\one_{P,P'\in\mathcal H}],
\end{align*}
where $P,P'$ are independent with law $D$.  Put $\bar q=1-q$.  Remaining-mass
accounting gives
\begin{align}
 F_{\mathsf{BE},D}(q)
 &= (1+\ell)\left(q-\frac{aq^2}{2}\right)
    +q\ell_M(\bar q+qd)+\frac{q^2K_M}{2}\notag\\
 &\quad+\mu\left(\bar q qd+\frac{\bar q^2}{2}\right)
    +\frac{q^2K_H}{2}.
 \label{eq:blind-F}
\end{align}
Atoms at $\tau_D$ are split between the low module and residual stock; atoms at
$\mu$ may be placed on either side of the blind block.  The same formula then
holds for the resulting virtual classes.

Each term in \cref{eq:blind-F} has a direct area interpretation.  During the
test module, work $(1+\ell)dq$ reduces unfinished mass from $1$ to $1-aq$.
The medium work $q\ell_M$ delays the untouched mass $\bar q$ and high mass $qd$, while
$K_M$ is the internal SPT pair term.  The blind block delays all high jobs and
itself loses mass linearly.  Finally $K_H$ is the high-tail pair term.
Expansion gives the quadratic
\begin{equation}
 F_{\mathsf{BE},D}(q)=\frac\mu2+Aq+Bq^2,
 \label{eq:blind-quadratic}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
 A&=1+\ell+\ell_M+\mu(d-1)
   =1-\EE(\mu-P)^+,
 \label{eq:blind-A}\\
 B&=-\frac{a(1+\ell)}2+\ell_M(d-1)+\frac{K_M}{2}
   +\mu\left(\frac12-d\right)+\frac{K_H}{2}.
 \label{eq:blind-B}
\end{align}
The envelope proof below implies $B\ge0$.  In the short-test regime
$\tau_D<\mu$, monotonicity in \cref{eq:common-threshold-equation} gives $A<0$, and
the optimal tested fraction is therefore
\begin{equation}
 q^*=\begin{cases}
  1,&B=0,\\
  \min\{1,-A/(2B)\},&B>0.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:blind-qstar}
\end{equation}
We define
\begin{equation}
 \PhiBE(D)=\min_{0\le q\le1}F_{\mathsf{BE},D}(q),
 \label{eq:blind-Phi}
\end{equation}
with $\PhiBE(D)=\mu/2$ in the regime $\tau_D\ge\mu$.

\subsection{The fixed-fraction completion envelope}

We first prove that \cref{eq:blind-four-block-order} is globally optimal in a
fluid relaxation.  Let $D$ have finite support
$0=p_0<p_1<\cdots<p_s\le L$, with masses $d_i$.  Fix a final tested fraction
$q$.  At an arbitrary schedule prefix, let
\begin{align*}
 t&=\text{tested mass},& b&=\text{blindly completed mass},\\
 c_i&=\text{processed tested mass of class }i>0.&&
\end{align*}
The completed mass and idealized work are
\begin{equation}
 y=d_0t+b+\sum_{i>0}c_i,\qquad
 x=t+\mu b+\sum_{i>0}p_ic_i,
 \label{eq:blind-fluid-work}
\end{equation}
subject to
\begin{equation}
 0\le t\le q,\qquad 0\le b\le1-q,\qquad
 0\le c_i\le d_it.
 \label{eq:blind-revelation-constraints}
\end{equation}
Define the model envelope
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_{\mathsf{BE},D,q}\defeq\mathcal C^\mu_{D,q},
 \label{eq:blind-model-envelope}
\end{equation}
using the common envelope \cref{eq:common-fluid-envelope}.  Every fluid schedule that ultimately tests
mass $q$ obeys this envelope, hence
\begin{equation}
 \cost\ge\int_0^\infty(1-\mathcal C_{\mathsf{BE},D,q}(x))\,dx.
 \label{eq:blind-envelope-lower}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-$q$ envelope]
\label{lem:blind-fixed-q-envelope}
If $\tau_D<\mu$, then for every $q\in[0,1]$ the right side of
\cref{eq:blind-envelope-lower} equals $F_{\mathsf{BE},D}(q)$ and is attained by the
four-block fluid policy.  If $\tau_D\ge\mu$, that right side is at least
$\mu/2$ for every $q$, with equality at $q=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem:test-module-envelope} with independent-mode work $v=\mu$.
The resulting item measure is
\[
                 qa\delta_{\tau_D}+q\nu+(1-q)\delta_\mu.
\]
When $\tau_D<\mu$, sorting these items by work per completion gives exactly
the low test module, residual classes below $\mu$, the blind module, and
residual classes above $\mu$, which is the four-block order
\cref{eq:blind-four-block-order}.  Expanding the common area formula
\cref{eq:common-envelope-area} across these four blocks gives
\cref{eq:blind-F}.

When $\tau_D\ge\mu$, every test and residual item costs at least $\mu$ per
completion.  The $q=0$ envelope, one blind item of capacity one and cost
$\mu$, therefore pointwise dominates every $q>0$ envelope and has area
$\mu/2$.  Finally, feasibility in
\cref{eq:blind-fluid-work,eq:blind-revelation-constraints} is affine in $q$,
so $\mathcal C_{\mathsf{BE},D,q}(x)$ is concave in $q$ for each $x$.  In the short-test regime its
area complement $F_{\mathsf{BE},D}(q)$ is convex, proving $B\ge0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Adaptive first touches are a uniform urn}
\label{sec:blind-adaptive-urn}

The fluid proof fixed $q$, but an announced finite policy may choose whom to
touch next and when to stop testing as arbitrary functions of its history.
The next finite lemma is the deferred-decisions statement needed to control
such a policy.

For a size-$n$ multiset whose values lie among
$0=p_0<p_1<\cdots<p_s\le L$, write $D_n$ for its empirical distribution,
$D_{n,i}=D_n(\{p_i\})$, and $\mu_n=\int p\,dD_n(p)$.

Distinguish occurrences even when their processing times coincide and fix a
deterministic proper policy, equivalently the private seed of a randomized
policy.  Apply \cref{lem:uniform-first-touch-event} to the $s+1$ classes with
failure probability $n^{-2}$, and put
$\Gamma_n=\max\{\Gamma_{\rm cell},\Gamma_{\rm work}\}$.  On the resulting
event, for every touch prefix leaving at least $\delta n$ labels untouched
and every class $i$,
\begin{align}
 |\#\text{tested class }i-D_{n,i}\#\text{tests}|
 &\le\Gamma_n,
 \label{eq:blind-tested-discrepancy}\\
 |\text{blind work}-\mu_n\#\text{blind jobs}|
 &\le\Gamma_n.
 \label{eq:blind-work-discrepancy}
\end{align}
Importantly, the common event does not condition on the eventual tested
fraction.

\subsection{The announced lower bound}
\label{sec:blind-announced}

Fix a policy seed.  Stop after $\lfloor(1-\delta)n\rfloor$ first touches and
let $Q_{n,\delta}$ be the number of tests completed by then, divided by $n$.
Edit the suffix: every still-untouched job that would later be tested is
instead executed blindly at its first original operation, and its later
processing record is deleted.  At most $\delta n+O(1)$ jobs and three
operations per affected job change.  The bounded suffix-edit lemma
\cref{lem:bounded-suffix-edit}, with $W=L+1$, gives
\begin{equation}
 O((L+1)\delta n^2).
 \label{eq:blind-suffix-edit}
\end{equation}
The edited schedule performs exactly $Q_{n,\delta}n$ tests.

Work on the single event
\cref{eq:blind-tested-discrepancy,eq:blind-work-discrepancy} and only now set
$q=Q_{n,\delta}$.  Normalize mass and work by $n$, and let
$\gamma_n=\Gamma_n/n$.  At any operation boundary, let $t,b,z,c_i$ denote
tested mass, blindly completed mass, zero mass completed by tests, and
processed known positive mass of class $i$.  The event gives
\[
 z\le D_{n,0}t+\gamma_n,\qquad
 c_i\le D_{n,i}t+\gamma_n.
\]
If $x$ is the actual normalized prefix work, the blind-work discrepancy and
the suffix edit give
\begin{equation}
 t+\mu_nb+\sum_i p_i(c_i-\gamma_n)^+
 \le x+(L+1)(\gamma_n+\delta).
 \label{eq:blind-work-repair}
\end{equation}
Thus all hypotheses of \cref{lem:cell-capacity-repair} hold with
$\alpha=(L+1)(\gamma_n+\delta)$, and it gives
\begin{equation}
 \text{completed fraction by }x
 \le \mathcal C_{\mathsf{BE},D_n,q}(x+(L+1)(\gamma_n+\delta))
      +(s+1)\gamma_n.
 \label{eq:blind-envelope-repair}
\end{equation}
The envelope horizon is at most $H=L+1$, and pure blind execution gives
$0\le\PhiBE(D_n)\le L/2$.  Apply
\cref{thm:completion-curve-transfer} with the suffix-edit error from
\cref{eq:blind-suffix-edit},
$\alpha=(L+1)(\gamma_n+\delta)$, and $\beta=(s+1)\gamma_n$.  It gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\cost(\mathcal A,M)}{n^2}
 \ge F_{\mathsf{BE},D_n}(Q_{n,\delta})
   -O_{L,s}(\gamma_n+\delta+1/n)
 \ge\PhiBE(D_n)-O_{L,s}(\gamma_n+\delta+1/n).
 \label{eq:blind-announced-finite-support}
\end{equation}
The long-test regime uses the $\mu_n/2$ dominance part of
\cref{lem:blind-fixed-q-envelope}; the same transfer applies.  Its finite-Yao
conclusion also proves the lower bound for randomized announced policies.

The random-labeling and announced minimax values coincide.  The transfer
lemma gives one inequality.  For the
other, privately relabel the physical jobs before running an optimal
deterministic urn policy; against every fixed assignment, the policy sees a
uniform assignment.  Thus the lower bound above applies to the announced
oblivious minimax problem, not merely to one randomized input model.
Write $\operatorname{VAL}_{\rm ann}(M)$ for this common minimax value.

\subsection{A growing grid and arbitrary bounded multisets}

The number of distinct values may grow with $n$.  Keep zero as a separate
cell, partition $(0,L]$ into
\begin{equation}
 K_n=\lfloor n^{1/6}\rfloor
 \label{eq:blind-grid-size}
\end{equation}
bins, and round every positive value upward; let $h_n=L/K_n$ be the mesh.
Zero preservation matters because a tested zero completes at its test.
Write $D[M]^{\rm grid}$ for the rounded empirical distribution and
$\mu_M^{\rm grid}=\int p\,dD[M]^{\rm grid}(p)$ for its mean.

Coupling each value with its rounded value and applying
\cref{lem:zero-preserving-transport} gives
\begin{equation}
 |\PhiBE(D)-\PhiBE(D^{\rm grid})|\le h_n.
 \label{eq:blind-rounding-stability}
\end{equation}
This avoids any assumption that the numerical optimizer or threshold is
stable under rounding.

Apply \cref{lem:uniform-first-touch-event} to all $K_n+1$ cells and take
$\delta_n=n^{-1/6}$.  The model-specific blind-work discrepancy supplies the
horizontal error; \cref{lem:cell-capacity-repair} supplies the vertical one.
After rounding, both are bounded by
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_n=O_L\!\left(
  \frac{(K_n+1)\Gamma_n}{n}+h_n+\delta_n\right)
 =O_L(n^{-1/6}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)}).
 \label{eq:blind-zeta}
\end{equation}
Consequently the rounded completion curve is bounded by
$\mathcal C_{\mathsf{BE},D[M]^{\rm grid},q}(x+\zeta_n)+\zeta_n$.
Apply \cref{thm:completion-curve-transfer} with $H=L+1$, $B=L/2$, the
suffix-edit error, and the failure probability from the urn lemma.  Together
with \cref{eq:blind-rounding-stability}, this yields the announced lower bound
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\operatorname{VAL}_{\rm ann}(M)}{n^2}
 \ge\PhiBE(D[M])-O_L(n^{-1/6}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)}).
 \label{eq:blind-announced-lower}
\end{equation}

For the matching upper bound, compute an optimal canonical template for the
rounded empirical multiset, choose $\lfloor q^*n\rfloor$ uniformly random
labels to test, and execute the four blocks.  Pairwise delay accounting
expresses its expectation as bounded one-occurrence and two-distinct-
occurrence kernels.  The without-replacement pair coefficients differ from
their product-law values by $O(1/n)$ uniformly in the tested count.  Hence
if $\pi$ records the virtual classes and the tested fraction,
\begin{equation}
 \EE C_n(\pi)=n^2F_{\mathsf{BE},D[M]^{\rm grid}}(\pi)+O_L(n)
 \label{eq:blind-finite-kernel}
\end{equation}
uniformly over canonical templates $\pi$.  Together with
\cref{eq:blind-rounding-stability}, this proves
\begin{equation}
 \left|\frac{\operatorname{VAL}_{\rm ann}(M)}{n^2}
       -\PhiBE(D[M])\right|
 \le O_L(n^{-1/6}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)}).
 \label{eq:blind-announced-sandwich}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Removing the announcement}

The universal policy first draws a uniform random permutation and blindly
executes
\begin{equation}
 k_n=\lceil n^{1/2}\rceil
 \label{eq:blind-pilot-size}
\end{equation}
sample jobs.  Their observed durations give a grid histogram $\widehat D_n$.
The policy minimizes the canonical quadratic over every grid threshold,
blind-block insertion position, and $q\in[0,1]$, then runs the selected
four-block template on the remaining jobs in the unused random order.

Let $D_n$ and $D_n^{\rm rem}$ be the full and remaining grid histograms.  Applying
\cref{eq:without-replacement-histogram} to the grid classes gives
\begin{align}
 \EE\|\widehat D_n-D_n\|_1
 \le\sqrt{\frac{K_n+1}{k_n}},
 &\qquad
 \EE\|\widehat D_n-D_n^{\rm rem}\|_1
 \le\frac{n}{n-k_n}\sqrt{\frac{K_n+1}{k_n}},\notag\\
 &\|D_n^{\rm rem}-D_n\|_1\le\frac{2k_n}{n-k_n}.
 \label{eq:blind-histogram-learning}
\end{align}
For a fixed canonical template, formula \cref{eq:blind-F} consists only of
bounded one- and two-draw expectations.  Therefore, uniformly in the grid
and template,
\begin{equation}
 |F_{\mathsf{BE},D}(\pi)-F_{\mathsf{BE},E}(\pi)|
 \le C_L\|D-E\|_1,
 \label{eq:blind-template-Lipschitz}
\end{equation}
for example with $C_L=20(L+1)$.  Let $\widehat\pi$ minimize the sampled
objective.
Conditional on the sample, the remaining order is uniform and the learned
template is fixed, so \cref{eq:blind-finite-kernel} applies.  The sample itself
has work at most $Lk_n$ and delays at most $n$ jobs, adding at most
$Lnk_n=O_L(n^{3/2})$.  Hence
\[
 \EE[\cost(\mathcal A^*,p)\mid\widehat D_n,D_n^{\rm rem}]
 \le n^2F_{\mathsf{BE},D_n^{\rm rem}}(\widehat\pi)+O_L(nk_n+n).
\]
Apply \cref{lem:pilot-learning-transfer} with
$d(D,E)=\|D-E\|_1$, $L_0=C_L$, and
\cref{eq:blind-histogram-learning}; then use
\cref{eq:blind-rounding-stability}.  This gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\EE\cost(\mathcal A^*,p)}{n^2}
 \le\PhiBE(D[M])
 +O_L\!\left(n^{-1/6}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)}\right)
 \label{eq:blind-unannounced-upper}
\end{equation}
uniformly over every fixed labeled vector $p\in[0,L]^n$.

For the converse, reveal $M$ to an arbitrary policy for free and draw a
uniform labeling.  The announced lower bound
\cref{eq:blind-announced-lower} applies seedwise.  Finite Yao selection
\cref{lem:finite-yao} gives a fixed labeling with cost at least the average,
which proves
\cref{eq:intro-blind-lower}.  This completes the proof of
\cref{thm:blind-instance}.

\subsection{Why the medium block is necessary}

Let
\[
 \Prob(P=0)=\frac15,\qquad
 \Prob(P=9)=\frac15,\qquad
 \Prob(P=16)=\frac35.
\]
Then $\mu=57/5$ and $\tau_D=5$.  Thus tested zeros are immediate, known
nine-jobs precede the blind block, and known sixteen-jobs follow it.  Formula
\cref{eq:blind-F} becomes
\[
 F_{\mathsf{BE},D}(q)=\frac{57}{10}-\frac{44}{25}q+\frac{11}{10}q^2.
\]
Its minimizer is $q^*=4/5$ and its value is $4.996$.  If all tested nonzero
jobs are instead deferred until after the blind block, the best value is
$5.04$.  The gap is a positive constant times $n^2$: the medium SPT block is
not cosmetic, and a policy of the simpler form ``threshold, then blind, then
all tested residuals'' is not instance optimal.
