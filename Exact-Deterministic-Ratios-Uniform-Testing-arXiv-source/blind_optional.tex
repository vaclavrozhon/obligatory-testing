\section{Blind execution: instance-optimal four-block algorithms}
\label{sec:blind}

We now turn to $\mathsf{BE}$.  Running an untouched job blindly consumes
its actual processing time and completes it; testing consumes one unit,
reveals the value, and permits the processing operation to be delayed.  The
adversary fixes a vector $p\in[0,L]^n$ before the private seed is drawn; the
private shuffle from the input convention then assigns its entries to labels.
The goal of this section is to prove
\cref{thm:blind-instance}, the rigorous version of the informal
blind-execution theorem \cref{thm:intro-blind-instance}.

\begin{theorem}[Blind-execution instance optimality]
\label{thm:blind-instance}
Fix $L<\infty$.  There exist a randomized nonanticipating algorithm $\mathcal A^*$,
which knows $n,L$ but not the entries of the input vector, and a deterministic
sequence $\eps_L(n)\to0$ with the following properties.  Every
$p\in[0,L]^n$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \EE\ALG_{\mathcal A^*}(p)
 \le n^2\PhiBE(D[p])+\eps_L(n)n^2.
 \label{eq:intro-blind-upper}
\end{equation}
Every $p\in[0,L]^n$ and every randomized algorithm $\mathcal A$, even an
announced algorithm, satisfy
\begin{equation}
 \EE\ALG_{\mathcal A}(p)
 \ge n^2\PhiBE(D[p])-\eps_L(n)n^2.
 \label{eq:intro-blind-lower}
\end{equation}
The benchmark $\PhiBE$ is defined explicitly in
\cref{sec:blind-benchmark}; it is the minimum of a one-variable quadratic.
One may take
$\eps_L(n)=O_L(n^{-1/6}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)})$.
\end{theorem}

The boundedness assumption in \cref{thm:blind-instance} cannot be removed.
In fact, without it even the much weaker worst-case guarantee is impossible.

\begin{theorem}[No finite ratio for unbounded blind execution]
\label{thm:blind-unbounded-impossible}
There are no randomized algorithms $\mathcal A_n$, constant $R<\infty$, and
sequence $\eta(n)\to0$ such that every vector
$p\in[0,\infty)^n$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \EE\ALG_{\mathcal A_n}(p)
 \le R\cdot\OPT(p)+\eta(n)n^2.
 \label{eq:blind-unbounded-impossible}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that such a guarantee exists, and run $\mathcal A_n$ on the all-zero
instance.  Its offline cost is zero, so if $Z_n$ denotes the expected online
cost, then $Z_n\le\eta(n)n^2=o(n^2)$.  We may assume that the algorithm finishes
every job, since otherwise its cost is infinite.

Fix its private seed and order the labels by their first touch on this zero
instance.  Every first touch completes its job: a blind zero takes no time,
while a tested zero completes with its unit test.  If the job at rank $h$ is
tested, that test is performed while $n-h+1$ jobs are unfinished and hence
contributes $n-h+1$ to the objective.  Consequently
\begin{equation}
 Z_n\ge\EE\sum_{h:\,\text{rank }h\text{ is tested}}(n-h+1).
 \label{eq:blind-zero-test-area}
\end{equation}

Now choose one label uniformly and change only its length from zero to
$H=n^2$.  Until this exceptional label is first touched, the algorithm sees
exactly the zero-instance history.  If its rank $h$ is touched blindly, its
nonpreemptive execution occupies the machine for $H$ time units while
$n-h+1$ jobs are unfinished.  Averaging over the exceptional label and the
private seed therefore gives
\begin{align}
 \EE\ALG_{\mathcal A_n}(p)
 &\ge \frac Hn\,\EE
   \sum_{h:\,\text{rank }h\text{ is blind}}(n-h+1)\notag\\
 &\ge\frac Hn\left(\frac{n(n+1)}2-Z_n\right)
  =H\left(\frac n2-o(n)\right).
 \label{eq:blind-one-long-average}
\end{align}
Some fixed choice of the exceptional label has at least this expected cost.
For every such labeling, the clairvoyant schedule runs all zero jobs first
and the long job last, so $\OPT(p)=H$.  With $H=n^2$,
\cref{eq:blind-one-long-average} contradicts
\cref{eq:blind-unbounded-impossible} for all sufficiently large $n$.
\end{proof}

The proof first groups a round of tests and the short outcomes processed
immediately after them into one divisible completion block.  For a fixed
tested fraction $q$, ordering all available blocks by work per completion
forces the four-block order from the introduction and gives its exact
quadratic cost.  For an adaptive algorithm, values exposed at first touch
still form a uniform permutation of the input, so one sampling event controls
all prefixes at once.  Discretizing $[0,L]$ extends the bound to arbitrary
bounded vectors, and an initial blind sample removes advance knowledge of the
input.

\subsection{The distributional benchmark}
\label{sec:blind-benchmark}

Let $D$ be a probability distribution on $[0,L]$.  Write
\begin{equation}
 \mu=\int p\,dD(p).
 \label{eq:blind-mean}
\end{equation}
Blind execution completes one unit of job mass using work $\mu$.
Apply \cref{lem:maximum-density-module} to $D$, choose its threshold selector
$g_*$, and let $\nu$ be the resulting residual measure.  The best possible
test-module density and its reciprocal are
\begin{equation}
 \kappa_D\defeq\max_g\frac{a_D(g)}{w_D(g)}=\frac1{\tau_D}.
 \label{eq:blind-density}
\end{equation}
The selector processes all positive values below $\tau_D$ immediately after
their tests, rejects all values above it, and may split probability mass
located exactly at the boundary.  With
that boundary convention, put
\[
 a=a_D(g_*),\qquad
 \ell=\int_{(0,L]}p g_*(p)\,dD(p).
\]
The identities in \cref{lem:maximum-density-module} give
\begin{equation}
 a\tau_D-\ell=1,
 \qquad \tau_D=\frac{1+\ell}{a}.
 \label{eq:blind-test-module}
\end{equation}
Thus a full unit of tests together with all selected low outcomes is a
divisible module of completion mass $a$, work $1+\ell$, and work per
completion $\tau_D$.

If $\tau_D\ge\mu$, blind work has at least the completion density of any test
module, and pure blind execution is optimal at the fluid scale.  Suppose now
that $\tau_D<\mu$.  After testing a fraction $q$, the resulting four-block
schedule is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
\underbrace{\text{tests with immediate }p<\tau_D}_{\text{test module}}
 &\ \longrightarrow\
 \underbrace{\text{known }\tau_D<p<\mu\text{ in SPT}}_{\text{medium}}\\[-2pt]
 &\ \longrightarrow\
 \underbrace{\text{untouched jobs blind}}_{\text{blind block}}
 \ \longrightarrow\
 \underbrace{\text{known }p>\mu\text{ in SPT}}_{\text{high tail}}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:blind-four-block-order}
\end{equation}

For the explicit cost, first assume no probability mass lies exactly at
$\tau_D$ or $\mu$, and put
\begin{align*}
 \mathcal L&=\{p<\tau_D\},&
 \mathcal M&=\{\tau_D<p<\mu\},&
 \mathcal H&=\{p>\mu\},\\
 a&=D(\mathcal L),&
 \ell&=\int_{\mathcal L}p\,dD(p),&
 \ell_M&=\int_{\mathcal M}p\,dD(p),\\
 d&=D(\mathcal H),&
 D_M&=D|_{\mathcal M},&D_H&=D|_{\mathcal H},
\end{align*}
where $D|_A$ denotes the restriction of $D$ to a set $A$.
Put $\bar q=1-q$.  Remaining-mass
accounting gives
\begin{align}
 F_{\mathsf{BE},D}(q)
 &= (1+\ell)\left(q-\frac{aq^2}{2}\right)
    +q\ell_M(\bar q+qd)+q^2\SPT(D_M)\notag\\
 &\quad+\mu\left(\bar q qd+\frac{\bar q^2}{2}\right)
    +q^2\SPT(D_H).
 \label{eq:blind-F}
\end{align}
Probability mass at $\tau_D$ is split between outcomes processed immediately
after their tests and the remaining tested jobs; mass at $\mu$ may be placed
on either side of the jobs run blindly.  The same formula then holds after
treating the two portions as separate classes.

Each term in \cref{eq:blind-F} has a direct area interpretation.  During the
test module, work $(1+\ell)dq$ reduces unfinished mass from $1$ to $1-aq$.
The medium work $q\ell_M$ delays the untouched mass $\bar q$ and high mass
$qd$, while $\SPT(D_M)$ is its internal divisible SPT cost.  The blind block
delays all high jobs and itself loses mass linearly.  Finally
$\SPT(D_H)$ is the high-tail SPT cost.
Expansion gives the quadratic
\begin{equation}
 F_{\mathsf{BE},D}(q)=\frac\mu2+Aq+Bq^2,
 \label{eq:blind-quadratic}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
 A&=1+\ell+\ell_M+\mu(d-1)
   =1-\int(\mu-p)^+\,dD(p),
 \label{eq:blind-A}\\
 B&=-\frac{a(1+\ell)}2+\ell_M(d-1)+\SPT(D_M)
   +\mu\left(\frac12-d\right)+\SPT(D_H).
 \label{eq:blind-B}
\end{align}
The envelope proof below implies $B\ge0$.  When testing has lower work per
completion than blind execution, namely $\tau_D<\mu$, monotonicity in
\cref{eq:common-threshold-equation} gives $A<0$, and
the optimal tested fraction is therefore
\begin{equation}
 q^*=\begin{cases}
  1,&B=0,\\
  \min\{1,-A/(2B)\},&B>0.
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:blind-qstar}
\end{equation}
We define
\begin{equation}
 \PhiBE(D)=\min_{0\le q\le1}F_{\mathsf{BE},D}(q),
 \label{eq:blind-Phi}
\end{equation}
with $\PhiBE(D)=\mu/2$ in the regime $\tau_D\ge\mu$.

\subsection{The fixed-fraction completion envelope}

We first prove that \cref{eq:blind-four-block-order} is globally optimal in a
fluid relaxation.  Let $D$ have finite support
$0=p_0<p_1<\cdots<p_s\le L$, with masses $d_i$.  Fix a final tested fraction
$q$.  At an arbitrary schedule prefix, let
\begin{align*}
 t&=\text{tested mass},& b&=\text{blindly completed mass},\\
 c_i&=\text{processed tested mass of class }i>0.&&
\end{align*}
The completed mass and idealized work are
\begin{equation}
 y=d_0t+b+\sum_{i>0}c_i,\qquad
 x=t+\mu b+\sum_{i>0}p_ic_i,
 \label{eq:blind-fluid-work}
\end{equation}
subject to
\begin{equation}
 0\le t\le q,\qquad 0\le b\le1-q,\qquad
 0\le c_i\le d_it.
 \label{eq:blind-revelation-constraints}
\end{equation}
Define the model envelope
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_{\mathsf{BE},D,q}\defeq\mathcal C^\mu_{D,q},
 \label{eq:blind-model-envelope}
\end{equation}
using the common envelope \cref{eq:common-fluid-envelope}.  Every fluid schedule that ultimately tests
mass $q$ obeys this envelope, hence
\begin{equation}
 \ALG\ge\Area(\mathcal C_{\mathsf{BE},D,q}).
 \label{eq:blind-envelope-lower}
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-$q$ envelope]
\label{lem:blind-fixed-q-envelope}
If $\tau_D<\mu$, then for every $q\in[0,1]$ the right side of
\cref{eq:blind-envelope-lower} equals $F_{\mathsf{BE},D}(q)$ and is attained by the
four-block fluid algorithm.  If $\tau_D\ge\mu$, that right side is at least
$\mu/2$ for every $q$, with equality at $q=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Apply \cref{lem:test-module-envelope} with work $v=\mu$ for jobs run blindly.
The resulting item measure is
\[
                 qa\delta_{\tau_D}+q\nu+(1-q)\delta_\mu.
\]
When $\tau_D<\mu$, sorting these items by work per completion gives exactly
the tests followed by selected low values, the remaining tested values below
$\mu$, the jobs run blindly, and the tested values above $\mu$.  This is the
four-block order
\cref{eq:blind-four-block-order}.  Expanding the common area formula
\cref{eq:common-envelope-area} across these four blocks gives
\cref{eq:blind-F}.

When $\tau_D\ge\mu$, every test and residual item costs at least $\mu$ per
completion.  The $q=0$ envelope, one blind item of capacity one and cost
$\mu$, therefore pointwise dominates every $q>0$ envelope and has area
$\mu/2$.  Finally, feasibility in
\cref{eq:blind-fluid-work,eq:blind-revelation-constraints} is affine in $q$,
so $\mathcal C_{\mathsf{BE},D,q}(x)$ is concave in $q$ for each $x$.  When
$\tau_D<\mu$, its
area complement $F_{\mathsf{BE},D}(q)$ is convex, proving $B\ge0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Sampling in the order jobs are first touched}
\label{sec:blind-adaptive-urn}

The fluid proof fixed $q$, but an announced finite algorithm may choose whom to
touch next and when to stop testing as arbitrary functions of its history.
The next argument uses the fact that, conditional on the history, the values
on untouched labels remain uniformly permuted.

For a size-$n$ vector whose coordinates lie among
$0=p_0<p_1<\cdots<p_s\le L$, write $D_n$ for its empirical distribution,
$D_{n,i}=D_n(\{p_i\})$, and $\mu_n=\sum_i p_iD_{n,i}$.

Treat jobs as distinct even when their processing times coincide and fix a
deterministic algorithm that completes every job, equivalently fix the
private seed of a randomized algorithm.  Let $X_k$ be the value encountered
at first touch $k$, and let $A_k$ indicate that the algorithm tests that job.
Deferred decisions make the $X_k$ a uniform permutation, and $A_k$ is
chosen before $X_k$ is exposed.  Apply
\cref{lem:predictable-permutation-sampling} first to the vector of class
indicators with selector $A_k$, and then to the scalar $X_k$ with selector
$1-A_k$, assigning failure probability $1/(2n^2)$ to each application.  Put
\begin{align*}
 \Gamma_{\rm cell}
  &=(1+\delta^{-1})E_{n,s+1,1,1/(2n^2)},\\
 \Gamma_{\rm work}
  &=(1+\delta^{-1})E_{n,1,L,1/(2n^2)},\\
 \Gamma_n&=\max\{\Gamma_{\rm cell},\Gamma_{\rm work}\}.
\end{align*}
On the common event, which has probability at least $1-n^{-2}$, every
prefix of the first-touch order that leaves at least $\delta n$ labels
untouched satisfies, for every class $i$,
\begin{align}
 |\#\text{tested class }i-D_{n,i}\#\text{tests}|
 &\le\Gamma_n,
 \label{eq:blind-tested-discrepancy}\\
 |\text{blind work}-\mu_n\#\text{blind jobs}|
 &\le\Gamma_n.
 \label{eq:blind-work-discrepancy}
\end{align}
Importantly, the common event does not condition on the eventual tested
fraction.

\subsection{The announced lower bound}
\label{sec:blind-announced}

Fix an algorithm seed.  Stop after $\lfloor(1-\delta)n\rfloor$ first touches and
let $Q_{n,\delta}$ be the number of tests completed by then, divided by $n$.
Edit the suffix: every still-untouched job that would later be tested is
instead executed blindly at its first original operation, and its later
processing record is deleted.  At most $\delta n+O(1)$ jobs and three
operations per affected job change.  Write completion cost as operation
length times the number of unfinished jobs.  The changed operations affect
the cost by $O((L+1)(\delta n^2+n))$; during every unchanged operation the
two unfinished-job counts differ by at most $\delta n+O(1)$, and the total
work is at most $(L+1)n$.  Thus
\begin{equation}
 \ALG_{\rm original}\ge
 \ALG_{\rm edited}-O((L+1)(\delta n^2+n)).
 \label{eq:blind-suffix-edit}
\end{equation}
The edited schedule performs exactly $Q_{n,\delta}n$ tests.

Work on the single event
\cref{eq:blind-tested-discrepancy,eq:blind-work-discrepancy} and only now set
$q=Q_{n,\delta}$.  Normalize mass and work by $n$, and let
$\gamma_n=\Gamma_n/n$.  At any operation boundary, let $t,b,z,c_i$ denote
tested mass, blindly completed mass, zero mass completed by tests, and
the processed mass of jobs whose revealed positive value belongs to class
$i$, respectively.  The event gives
\[
 z\le D_{n,0}t+\gamma_n,\qquad
 c_i\le D_{n,i}t+\gamma_n.
\]
If $x$ is the normalized work performed so far, the bound
\cref{eq:blind-work-discrepancy} and the modification of the final
$\delta n+O(1)$ jobs give
\begin{equation}
 t+\mu_nb+\sum_i p_i(c_i-\gamma_n)^+
 \le x+(L+1)(\gamma_n+\delta).
 \label{eq:blind-work-repair}
\end{equation}
Set $c_i'=(c_i-\gamma_n)^+$.  We also have $t\le q$ and $b\le1-q$, so
$t,b,c_i'$ satisfy the constraints defining
$\mathcal C_{\mathsf{BE},D_n,q}$ at work
$x+(L+1)(\gamma_n+\delta)$.  Replacing $z,c_i$ by
$D_{n,0}t,c_i'$ removes at most $(s+1)\gamma_n$ completed mass.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \text{completed fraction by }x
 \le \mathcal C_{\mathsf{BE},D_n,q}(x+(L+1)(\gamma_n+\delta))
      +(s+1)\gamma_n.
 \label{eq:blind-envelope-repair}
\end{equation}
The envelope reaches completed mass one by normalized work at most $H=L+1$,
so integrating \cref{eq:blind-envelope-repair} loses at most
$(L+1)(\gamma_n+\delta)+H(s+1)\gamma_n$ from its fluid area.  By
\cref{lem:blind-fixed-q-envelope}, this area is
$F_{\mathsf{BE},D_n}(q)$ when $\tau_{D_n}<\mu_n$ and is at least
$\mu_n/2$ otherwise; in either case it is at least $\PhiBE(D_n)$.  The
sampling event fails with probability at most $n^{-2}$, and pure blind
execution gives $0\le\PhiBE(D_n)\le L/2$.  Averaging and using
\cref{eq:blind-suffix-edit} now gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\EE\ALG_{\mathcal A}(p)}{n^2}
 \ge\PhiBE(D_n)-O_{L,s}(\gamma_n+\delta+1/n).
 \label{eq:blind-announced-finite-support}
\end{equation}
The estimate
is uniform in the fixed seed and therefore remains valid after averaging
over a randomized announced algorithm.

Write $\operatorname{VAL}_{\rm ann}(p)$ for the minimum expected cost of an
announced algorithm under the private-shuffle convention.

\subsection{A growing grid and arbitrary bounded vectors}

The number of distinct values may grow with $n$.  Keep zero as a separate
cell, partition $(0,L]$ into
\begin{equation}
 K_n=\lfloor n^{1/6}\rfloor
 \label{eq:blind-grid-size}
\end{equation}
bins, and round every positive value upward; let $h_n=L/K_n$ be the mesh.
Zero preservation matters because a tested zero completes at its test.
Write $D[p]^{\rm grid}$ for the rounded empirical distribution and
$\mu_p^{\rm grid}=\int p\,dD[p]^{\rm grid}(p)$.

Coupling each value with its rounded value and applying
\cref{lem:zero-preserving-transport} gives
\begin{equation}
 |\PhiBE(D)-\PhiBE(D^{\rm grid})|\le h_n.
 \label{eq:blind-rounding-stability}
\end{equation}
This avoids any assumption that the numerical optimizer or threshold is
stable under rounding.

Take $\delta_n=n^{-1/6}$ and repeat the two predictable-sampling applications
above for the $K_n+1$ grid cells, defining $\Gamma_n$ as the larger of their
two bounds.  At any prefix of the edited schedule, subtracting
$\Gamma_n/n$ from the completed mass in each positive class makes all class
capacities exact.  The blind-work discrepancy, the edited suffix, and upward
rounding shift normalized work by at most the same order.  Hence both the
horizontal work error and the total removed completion mass are bounded by
\begin{equation}
 \zeta_n=O_L\!\left(
  \frac{(K_n+1)\Gamma_n}{n}+h_n+\delta_n\right)
 =O_L(n^{-1/6}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)}).
 \label{eq:blind-zeta}
\end{equation}
Consequently the rounded completion curve is bounded by
$\mathcal C_{\mathsf{BE},D[p]^{\rm grid},q}(x+\zeta_n)+\zeta_n$.
Integrating this inequality up to normalized work $H=L+1$ loses at most
$(H+1)\zeta_n$ from the rounded fluid area.  The first-touch event fails with
probability $n^{-2}$, whose contribution is at most
$n^{-2}L/2$, and the suffix edit has the cost established in
\cref{eq:blind-suffix-edit}.  Together with
\cref{eq:blind-rounding-stability}, this yields the announced lower bound
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\operatorname{VAL}_{\rm ann}(p)}{n^2}
 \ge\PhiBE(D[p])-O_L(n^{-1/6}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)}).
 \label{eq:blind-announced-lower}
\end{equation}

For the matching upper bound, choose the optimal split of boundary classes
and tested fraction for the rounded empirical vector; denote these choices
by $\pi$.  Select $\lfloor q^*n\rfloor$ labels uniformly to test and execute
the four blocks.  Expanding every delay into contributions of individual
jobs and unordered pairs expresses its expectation as a sum of bounded
terms.  For distinct jobs, the probability that zero, one, or two members of
a pair are tested differs from the corresponding independent probability by
$O(1/n)$, uniformly in the tested count.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \EE C_n(\pi)=n^2F_{\mathsf{BE},D[p]^{\rm grid}}(\pi)+O_L(n)
 \label{eq:blind-finite-kernel}
\end{equation}
uniformly over choices $\pi$.  Together with
\cref{eq:blind-rounding-stability}, this proves
\begin{equation}
 \left|\frac{\operatorname{VAL}_{\rm ann}(p)}{n^2}
       -\PhiBE(D[p])\right|
 \le O_L(n^{-1/6}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)}).
 \label{eq:blind-announced-sandwich}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Removing the announcement}

The universal algorithm first draws a uniform random permutation and blindly
executes
\begin{equation}
 k_n=\lceil n^{1/2}\rceil
 \label{eq:blind-pilot-size}
\end{equation}
sample jobs.  Their observed durations give a grid histogram $\widehat D_n$.
The algorithm minimizes the quadratic \cref{eq:blind-quadratic} over every grid threshold,
position at which the jobs run blindly are inserted, and $q\in[0,1]$, then runs the selected
four-block template on the remaining jobs in the unused random order.

Let $D_n$ and $D_n^{\rm rem}$ be the full and remaining grid histograms.  Applying
\cref{eq:without-replacement-histogram} to the grid classes gives
\begin{align}
 \EE\|\widehat D_n-D_n\|_1
 \le\sqrt{\frac{K_n+1}{k_n}},
 &\qquad
 \EE\|\widehat D_n-D_n^{\rm rem}\|_1
 \le\frac{n}{n-k_n}\sqrt{\frac{K_n+1}{k_n}},\notag\\
 &\|D_n^{\rm rem}-D_n\|_1\le\frac{2k_n}{n-k_n}.
 \label{eq:blind-histogram-learning}
\end{align}
For fixed scheduling choices $\pi$, formula \cref{eq:blind-F} consists only
of bounded single and double integrals.  Therefore, uniformly in the grid
and in $\pi$,
\begin{equation}
 |F_{\mathsf{BE},D}(\pi)-F_{\mathsf{BE},E}(\pi)|
 \le C_L\|D-E\|_1,
 \label{eq:blind-template-Lipschitz}
\end{equation}
for example with $C_L=20(L+1)$.  Let $\widehat\pi$ minimize the sampled
objective.
Conditional on the sample, the remaining order is uniform and the learned
template is fixed, so \cref{eq:blind-finite-kernel} applies.  The sample itself
has work at most $Lk_n$ and delays at most $n$ jobs, adding at most
$Lnk_n=O_L(n^{3/2})$.  Hence
\[
 \EE[\ALG_{\mathcal A^*}(p)\mid\widehat D_n,D_n^{\rm rem}]
 \le n^2F_{\mathsf{BE},D_n^{\rm rem}}(\widehat\pi)+O_L(nk_n+n).
\]
The template Lipschitz bound, applied first to $\widehat\pi$ and then to an
optimal template for $D_n^{\rm rem}$, gives
\[
 F_{\mathsf{BE},D_n^{\rm rem}}(\widehat\pi)
 \le \PhiBE(D_n^{\rm rem})
      +2C_L\|\widehat D_n-D_n^{\rm rem}\|_1.
\]
Applying it once more to an optimal template for $D_n$ gives
$\PhiBE(D_n^{\rm rem})\le
\PhiBE(D_n)+C_L\|D_n^{\rm rem}-D_n\|_1$.
Average, use \cref{eq:blind-histogram-learning}, and then use the rounding
bound \cref{eq:blind-rounding-stability}.  This gives
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\EE\ALG_{\mathcal A^*}(p)}{n^2}
 \le\PhiBE(D[p])
 +O_L\!\left(n^{-1/6}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)}\right)
 \label{eq:blind-unannounced-upper}
\end{equation}
uniformly over every vector $p\in[0,L]^n$.

For the converse, reveal the entries of $p$ to an arbitrary algorithm for free.
The announced lower bound \cref{eq:blind-announced-lower} applies seedwise
and, after averaging over the seed, proves
\cref{eq:intro-blind-lower}.  This completes the proof of
\cref{thm:blind-instance}.

\subsection{Why some tested jobs must come before blind execution}

Let
\[
 D(\{0\})=\frac15,\qquad
 D(\{9\})=\frac15,\qquad
 D(\{16\})=\frac35.
\]
Then $\mu=57/5$ and $\tau_D=5$.  Thus tested zeros complete during their
tests, tested jobs with $p=9$ precede the jobs run blindly, and tested jobs
with $p=16$ follow them.  Formula
\cref{eq:blind-F} becomes
\[
 F_{\mathsf{BE},D}(q)=\frac{57}{10}-\frac{44}{25}q+\frac{11}{10}q^2.
\]
Its minimizer is $q^*=4/5$ and its value is $4.996$.  If all tested nonzero
jobs are instead deferred until after the blind block, the best value is
$5.04$.  The gap is a positive constant times $n^2$: processing the tested
jobs with $\tau_D<p<\mu$ before the jobs run blindly is necessary.  An
algorithm of the simpler form ``threshold, then blind, then all tested
residuals'' is not instance optimal.
