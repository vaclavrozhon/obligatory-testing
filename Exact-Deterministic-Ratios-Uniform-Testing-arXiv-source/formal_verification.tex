\section{Machine-checked verification}
\label{sec:formalization}

The accompanying Lean~4 development~\cite{DeMouraUllrich2021} uses
mathlib~\cite{Mathlib2020} and is pinned to version~4.30.0.  Its root module
imports the complete deterministic, randomized-obligatory, and
blind-execution developments.  The source contains no \texttt{sorry},
\texttt{admit}, or project-defined axiom.  This section records the precise
proof boundary; \cref{app:lean-formalization} gives the detailed dictionary
for the deterministic exact curve.  The revealing-optimization instance theorem of
\cref{sec:common-upper-instance} and its randomized curve in
\cref{sec:random-common-upper} are currently analytic only and are not
included in the machine-checked claims below.  The blind-optimization
instance theorem and exact curve of \cref{sec:blind-optimization} are also
currently analytic only.

\subsection{Deterministic exact ratios}

The theorem \texttt{SchedulingPaper.exact\_ratio\_main} packages both
directions of the six-piece finite-cap curve and the obligatory limit.
Its upper fields contain literal transcript-only strategies, termination
proofs, and $O(n)$ certificates; its lower fields quantify over arbitrary
deterministic strategy families and return genuine fixed inputs after the
formal replay theorem.  The formal curve includes all five joins, continuity,
the plateau, and the global maximum.

\subsection{Randomized obligatory testing}

The module \texttt{SchedulingPaper.RandomizedFourThirds} exports the
operational upper theorem
\begin{lstlisting}[style=lean]
theorem unknownMultiset_operational_expectedCost_le_20378 ... :
  uniformAverage (physicalSampledRunCost ...) <=
    4 / 3 * finiteObligatoryOPT p + 20378 * R ^ 7
\end{lstlisting}
At the concrete parameter choice $n\le R^4$, this is
\cref{eq:rand-final-upper}.  The proof below this interface includes the
maximum-density optimizer, exact and robust $4/3$ certificates, threshold
closure for sample-empty bins, finite hypergeometric concentration, the
cutoff-$32$ fallback, sample-first accounting, bad-event excess, and
termination of the sampled transcript strategy.

The same module exports
\texttt{oblivious\_iid\_binary\_lower\_online\_actualOPT}.  It accepts an
arbitrary finite family of public-transcript strategies, assumes only that
the supplied fuel completes every fair binary input, and returns a fixed
binary input whose average cost has coefficient $4n/(3n+5)$.  The formal
development identifies its denominator with the literal clairvoyant
obligatory optimum.  Since $4n/(3n+5)\to4/3$, the upper and lower exports give
the machine-checked sharp asymptotic value.

The instance-optimal variant in \cref{sec:obligatory-instance} replaces the
checked cutoff $32$ by the slowly growing cutoff from
\cref{eq:ot-learning-parameters}.  Its full assembly is currently analytic.
Lean verifies the shared
finite stationary-cost identity, maximum-density algebra, and fluid
$4/3$ certificate in \texttt{RandomizedAnnouncedFluid} and the randomized
operational modules.  It does not currently export the whole-measure
quantile transfer \cref{eq:ot-quantile-transfer} or an end-to-end theorem for
\cref{thm:obligatory-instance}; those are not included in the
machine-checked claim.

\subsection{Blind-execution instance optimality}

This formalization uses an operation semantics in which completion of a blind
job records its realized duration.  The trace-bijection and observed-trace
modules prove the occurrence-token reveal bijection of
\cref{lem:blind-trace-bijection}, its lockstep causality theorem, the
predictable test selector, and the exact selected-count and blind-work
identities.

The deterministic analytic layer checks the full-module replacement,
fractional-knapsack envelope, long-test dominance, revelation repair,
horizontal integral shift, formula \cref{eq:blind-F}, its quadratic normal
form and vertex minimizer, and the three-point witness.  The finite layer
checks all-grid repair, bounded one- and two-job kernels, histogram stability,
the canonical physical operation word, a blind-pilot compiler, and the
announced adaptive lower bound.

The end-to-end theorem is exported as
\texttt{boundedUniform\_blindPilot\_concrete\_rate} in
\texttt{RandomizedOptionalUnknownRates}.  In mathematical notation, for
$m=\lfloor n^{1/16}\rfloor\ge2$, every $p\in[0,L]^n$, and every complete
announced policy $\pi$, it proves
\begin{equation}
 \frac{\EE C_{\rm learned}(p)}{n^2}
 \le \frac{\EE C_\pi(p)}{n^2}
    +\frac{7830(L+1)^2}{m}.
 \label{eq:lean-blind-rate}
\end{equation}
The learned algorithm depends only on $n,L$ and its private permutations,
not on the multiset.  Lean separately proves
$\lfloor n^{1/16}\rfloor\to\infty$ and hence that the displayed error tends
to zero.  It also discharges the zero-mean case for the same algorithm.  This
is a slower rate than the exponential-tail analysis in
\cref{eq:blind-unannounced-upper}, but it proves the same uniform
$o_L(n^2)$ theorem using finite second-moment and checkpoint bounds.

The formal statements use finite uniform averages rather than measure-valued
random seeds.  This is sufficient for the advertised algorithms and Yao
distributions, all of which use finite permutations, finite pilot choices,
and finite tie-breaking coins.  Classical real analysis and noncomputable
exact minimizers are used in certificates; no floating-point program is
extracted.
