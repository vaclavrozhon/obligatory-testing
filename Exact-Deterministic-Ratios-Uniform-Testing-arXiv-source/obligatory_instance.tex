\section{Obligatory-testing instance optimality}
\label{sec:obligatory-instance}

The goal of this section is to prove \cref{thm:obligatory-instance}, the
rigorous version of the ``one algorithm for every input'' statement
\cref{thm:intro-obligatory-instance}.  The algorithm is not a new one: it is
a slight variant of the policy from \cref{sec:random-obligatory}, obtained by
replacing its fixed cutoff $32$ by a slowly growing cutoff.  The new point is
that this variant attains, on every bounded input, the best leading cost
available even to a policy that is told the input in advance.

For a multiset $M=\{p_1,\ldots,p_n\}$, write $D_M$ for its empirical
probability measure.  We define below an explicit number
$\PhiOT(D_M)$.  It is obtained by scanning the processing times in
increasing order, choosing the prefix with the largest number of completions
per unit of discovery work, and evaluating the stationary policy for that
prefix.

\begin{theorem}[Obligatory-testing instance optimality]
\label{thm:obligatory-instance}
There is a single randomized nonanticipating policy $\mathcal A^*$, depending
only on $n$ and not on the multiset, with the following property.  For every
fixed $L<\infty$ there is a deterministic sequence
$\eps_L(n)\to0$ such that, for every size-$n$ multiset
$M\subseteq[0,L]$ and every fixed labeling $\sigma$,
\begin{equation}
 \EE\cost(\mathcal A^*,\sigma(M))
 \le n^2\PhiOT(D_M)+\eps_L(n)n^2.
 \label{eq:ot-instance-upper}
\end{equation}
Conversely, for every randomized policy $\mathcal A$, even one told $M$ in
advance, some fixed oblivious labeling $\sigma$ satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \EE\cost(\mathcal A,\sigma(M))
 \ge n^2\PhiOT(D_M)-\eps_L(n)n^2.
 \label{eq:ot-instance-lower}
\end{equation}
One may take $\eps_L(n)=O_L(n^{-1/5})$.  Thus $\mathcal A^*$ is
asymptotically instance-optimal, uniformly over all bounded inputs.
\end{theorem}

The upper estimate will have order $O_L(n^{-1/5})$.  The announced lower
bound is sharper, of order $O_L(n^{-1/2}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)})$; we use the slower
common rate only to keep the theorem statement symmetric.

\begin{theorem}[When instance optimality is impossible]
\label{thm:ot-instance-impossibility}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textnormal{(\roman*)}]
\item There is no deterministic instance-optimal policy, even when all job
lengths lie in $[0,2]$.  More precisely, for every deterministic policy
$\mathcal A$, even one told the input, and every even $n$, there is a fixed
labeling of the input with $n/2$ zero jobs and $n/2$ length-two jobs for which
\begin{equation}
 \cost(\mathcal A)\ge\frac98n^2.
 \label{eq:ot-det-not-instance-optimal}
\end{equation}
On the same input, a randomized policy $\mathcal A'$ that is told the input has
\[
 \cost(\mathcal A')=n^2+\frac34n.
\]
Thus $\cost(\mathcal A)>\cost(\mathcal A')+n^2/8-O(n)$.

\item If job lengths are not bounded by a fixed constant, there is no
randomized instance-optimal policy with one uniform additive $o(n^2)$ term.
More precisely, for every randomized policy $\mathcal A$ independent of the
input and every sufficiently large $n$, there is an input with lengths in
$[0,n^2]$, a fixed labeling, and a randomized policy $\mathcal A'$ told that
input such that
\begin{equation}
 \cost(\mathcal A)\ge\cost(\mathcal A')+\frac18n^2.
 \label{eq:ot-unbounded-not-instance-optimal}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
As in the introduction, $\cost(\mathcal A)$ includes the expectation over
the private randomness of $\mathcal A$.  For part~(i), write $n=2m$.
Against the fixed deterministic policy, reveal
length two on the first $m$ labels it tests and zero on the remaining $m$.
As usual, replay freezes this interaction into one fixed oblivious labeling.
If the policy does not finish, the claim is immediate.  Otherwise, let $r$
be the number of length-two jobs completed before the first zero is tested.
By that time all $m$ positive jobs have been tested, so at least $m+2r$ work
has elapsed.  The first $r$ completed positive jobs contribute at least
$3r(r+1)/2$.  At that point the remaining jobs consist of $m$ unit tests of
zero jobs and $m-r$ processing operations of length two.  Even SPT order for
this remaining work gives
\begin{align*}
 \cost(\mathcal A,M)
 &\ge \frac{3r(r+1)}2
 +(2m-r)(m+2r)+\frac{m(m+1)}2\\
 &\hspace{22mm}{}+m(m-r)+(m-r)(m-r+1)\\
 &=\frac92m^2+\frac12r^2+\frac32m+\frac12r
 \ge\frac98n^2.
\end{align*}
The comparison policy $\mathcal A'$ tests in a uniformly random order, completes zeros at
their tests, defers every length-two job, and finally processes the deferred
jobs.  This is the stationary policy with the zero jobs early.  Its exact
cost identity \cref{eq:rand-stationary-cost} gives
\[
 \cost(\mathcal A')=n^2+\frac34n.
\]
This proves the first claim.

For part~(ii), put $H=n^2$ and first run $\mathcal A$ on the input $M^0$ in
which every job has length $H$.  For a fixed private seed, let $X_k$ be the
number of completed jobs immediately before the $k$th test.  Every processing
operation contributes the same $Hn(n+1)/2$ in total, regardless of its
position.  The $k$th test delays $n-X_k$ unfinished jobs.  Hence the excess
over the policy $\mathcal A'$ that tests and processes each job
immediately satisfies
\begin{equation}
 \cost(\mathcal A)-\cost(\mathcal A')
 =\EE\sum_{k=1}^n(k-1-X_k).
 \label{eq:ot-all-H-excess}
\end{equation}
If this sum has expectation at least $n^2/8$, we are done.

Otherwise, replace one uniformly random label by a zero and leave every
other length equal to $H$; call the resulting input $M^1$.  Conditional on the
seed, the rank $K$ at which the zero label is tested is uniform on
$\{1,\ldots,n\}$.  Until that test, the run agrees with the all-$H$ run, and
therefore $X_K$ length-$H$ jobs complete before the zero.  From
\cref{eq:ot-all-H-excess},
\begin{equation}
 \EE X_K
 =\frac1n\EE\sum_{k=1}^nX_k
 >\frac{n-1}{2}-\frac n8
 =\frac{3n}{8}-\frac12.
 \label{eq:ot-zero-rank-completions}
\end{equation}
Forcing the zero job behind $X_K$ length-$H$ jobs adds $HX_K$ to the
clairvoyant SPT lower bound.  On the other hand, a policy told $M^1$ can test
in a private random order, defer positive jobs until it finds the zero, and
then finish the positive jobs.  Its completion order is SPT, and moving its
unit tests away from the corresponding processing operations costs at most
$n^2$.  Thus
\[
 \cost(\mathcal A)-\cost(\mathcal A')
 \ge H\left(\frac{3n}{8}-\frac12\right)-n^2.
\]
For $H=n^2$ this exceeds $n^2/8$ for all sufficiently large $n$.  Averaging
over the possible zero labels shows that one fixed oblivious labeling has
the same lower bound.  This proves part~(ii).
\end{proof}

\subsection{The maximum-density benchmark}

We first define the benchmark for an arbitrary finitely supported
probability distribution $D$ on $[0,\infty)$.  Apply the maximum-density
module \cref{lem:maximum-density-module}, choose its threshold selector
$g_*$, and put
\begin{equation}
 a_*=a_D(g_*),\qquad w_*=w_D(g_*),\qquad
 \rho_* =\frac{a_*}{w_*},\qquad
 \theta_*=\frac1{\rho_*}=\tau_D.
 \label{eq:ot-module-threshold}
\end{equation}
The residual measure $\nu$ from \cref{eq:common-module-data} has mass
$1-a_*$ and support in $[\theta_*,\infty)$.  For an empirical measure one
may equivalently sort the occurrences and maximize
\begin{equation}
 \frac{|E|/n}{1+n^{-1}\sum_{i\in E}p_i}
 \label{eq:ot-empirical-density}
\end{equation}
over processing-time prefixes $E$ containing all zeros.  A tied boundary
class may be placed on either side.

Define
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \PhiOT(D)
 =w_*\left(1-\frac{a_*}{2}\right)
  +\frac12\iint\min\{p,q\}\,d\nu(p)d\nu(q).}
 \label{eq:ot-Phi}
\end{equation}
For $D=D_M$ and a whole-occurrence maximizing prefix $E$, this is precisely
$P(p,E)$ from \cref{eq:rand-P}.  The next lemma proves both that the value is
independent of choices at a tied atom and that it lower-bounds every fluid
policy.

\begin{lemma}[Pointwise completion envelope]
\label{lem:ot-completion-envelope}
For scaled time $s\ge0$, let
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal C_D(s)=\max\left\{
  D(\{0\})T+\sum_{p>0}c_p:
  \begin{array}{l}
   0\le T\le1,\quad 0\le c_p\le D(\{p\})T,\\
   T+\sum_{p>0}p c_p\le s
  \end{array}\right\}.
 \label{eq:ot-envelope}
\end{equation}
Then $\mathcal C_D$ is attained simultaneously at all times by the stationary
maximum-density schedule: repeat infinitesimal full modules $g_*$ until all
jobs have been tested, then process the residual measure $\nu$ in SPT order.
Moreover,
\begin{equation}
 \PhiOT(D)=\int_0^\infty(1-\mathcal C_D(s))\,ds.
 \label{eq:ot-Phi-area}
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The envelope in \cref{eq:ot-envelope} is
$\mathcal C^v_{D,1}$ from \cref{eq:common-fluid-envelope}; the independent-mode term
has zero capacity, so $v$ is immaterial.  By
\cref{lem:test-module-envelope}, its item measure is
\[
                         a_*\delta_{\theta_*}+\nu.
\]
The greedy realization first repeats the full maximum-density module and
then processes $\nu$ in SPT order, proving the pointwise assertion.  Since
$\nu$ has mass $1-a_*$ and is supported above $\theta_*$, the common area
formula \cref{eq:common-envelope-area} expands to
\begin{align*}
 \int_0^\infty(1-\mathcal C_D(s))\,ds
 &=\theta_*\left(\frac{a_*^2}{2}+a_*(1-a_*)\right)
   +\frac12\iint\min\{p,q\}\,d\nu(p)d\nu(q)\\
 &=w_*\left(1-\frac{a_*}{2}\right)
   +\frac12\iint\min\{p,q\}\,d\nu(p)d\nu(q),
\end{align*}
because $w_*=a_*\theta_*$.  This is \cref{eq:ot-Phi} and is unaffected by
how a boundary atom is split.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The announced upper bound}

Suppose the input is announced but its labeling is not.  Choose a maximizing
whole-occurrence prefix $E$, test labels in a uniformly random order, process
an $E$-job immediately, and finish the residual jobs in SPT order.  The exact
identity \cref{eq:rand-stationary-cost} and
\cref{eq:ot-Phi} give
\begin{equation}
 \EE\cost(\mathrm{stat},M)
 =n^2\PhiOT(D_M)
  +\frac12\left(|E|+\sum_i p_i\right).
 \label{eq:ot-announced-upper}
\end{equation}
In particular, if $M\subseteq[0,L]$, the remainder is at most
$(1+L)n/2$.  The announced optimum is therefore at most the claimed fluid
value plus $O_L(n)$.

\subsection{No adaptive announced policy has a better leading cost}

Assign the occurrences of $M$ uniformly to the public labels and fix a
deterministic nonanticipating policy.  In the adaptive order in which it
tests new labels, the revealed occurrences form a uniform random permutation
$P_1,\ldots,P_n$ of $M$.  One way to see this without assuming distinct
processing times is to give equal-valued occurrences temporary private
tokens: the map from a placement to the order in which the policy first
reveals these tokens is a bijection of the finite permutation space.

For every prefix length $k$ and threshold $z$, put
\begin{equation}
 N_k(z)=|\{j\le k:P_j\le z\}|,\qquad
 N(z)=|\{i:p_i\le z\}|,
\end{equation}
and define
\begin{equation}
 \Delta=\max_{0\le k\le n}\max_z
 \left|N_k(z)-\frac{k}{n}N(z)\right|.
 \label{eq:ot-prefix-discrepancy}
\end{equation}
Only the at most $n+1$ thresholds immediately after values of $M$ matter.
Apply the uniform-prefix sampling bound
\cref{eq:uniform-prefix-hoeffding,eq:uniform-prefix-mean} to the binary
indicators $\one_{p_i\le z}$ and union-bound over these thresholds.  This
gives universal constants $c,C>0$ such that
\begin{equation}
 \Prob(\Delta\ge u)\le2(n+1)^2e^{-cu^2/n},\qquad
 \EE\Delta\le C\sqrt{n\ln(n+2)}.
 \label{eq:ot-discrepancy-concentration}
\end{equation}

We now transfer a realized schedule prefix to the deterministic fluid
envelope.  For a finite subprobability measure $\lambda$, let
\begin{equation}
 Q_\lambda(u)=\inf\{z:\lambda([0,z])\ge u\},\qquad
 W_\lambda(c)=\int_0^cQ_\lambda(u)\,du.
 \label{eq:ot-quantile-work}
\end{equation}
Thus $W_\lambda(c)$ is the least work needed to process mass $c$ from
$\lambda$, with the endpoint fraction of an atom taken when necessary.
After $k$ tests define
\begin{equation}
 R_k=\frac1n\sum_{j\le k}\delta_{P_j},\qquad
 A_k=\frac{k}{n}D_M,\qquad \delta=\frac\Delta n.
\end{equation}
Both measures have mass $k/n$, and
\begin{equation}
 \sup_z|R_k([0,z])-A_k([0,z])|\le\delta.
 \label{eq:ot-cdf-transfer}
\end{equation}
Consequently, for $u>\delta$,
$Q_{A_k}(u-\delta)\le Q_{R_k}(u)$.  Indeed, whenever the $R_k$-CDF has
reached $u$, \cref{eq:ot-cdf-transfer} says that the $A_k$-CDF has reached
$u-\delta$; taking generalized inverses gives the inequality, including at
atom endpoints.  Thus, when $c\ge\delta$, integration yields
\begin{equation}
 W_{A_k}((c-\delta)_+)
 \le\int_\delta^cQ_{R_k}(u)\,du
 \le W_{R_k}(c).
 \label{eq:ot-quantile-transfer}
\end{equation}
If $c<\delta$, the retained mass $(c-\delta)_+$ is zero and the corresponding
claim is immediate.

At physical time $t$, suppose $k$ tests have completed, the policy has
completed mass $c$ of jobs, and the positive processing work belonging to
those completed jobs is $nq$.  Replacing the completed submeasure by the
shortest mass $c$ of $R_k$ can only reduce its work, so
$W_{R_k}(c)\le q$.  Also $k+nq\le t$.  By
\cref{eq:ot-quantile-transfer}, the proportional supply $A_k$ contains mass
$(c-\delta)_+$ processable with work at most $q$.  This is a feasible point
of \cref{eq:ot-envelope} at scaled time $t/n$.  Therefore, pathwise and
simultaneously for every $t$,
\begin{equation}
 \#\{\text{jobs completed by time }t\}
 \le nF_{D_M}(t/n)+\Delta.
 \label{eq:ot-pointwise-completion}
\end{equation}

Put $H=1+n^{-1}\sum_i p_i$.  The fluid curve is one by time $H$.  Integrating
the unfinished-job area in \cref{eq:ot-pointwise-completion} gives the
pathwise inequality
\begin{align}
 \cost(\mathcal A,M)
 &\ge\int_0^{nH}
   \left[n(1-F_{D_M}(t/n))-\Delta\right]dt\notag\\
 &=n^2\PhiOT(D_M)-Hn\Delta.
 \label{eq:ot-announced-lower-pathwise}
\end{align}
Taking expectations and using
\cref{eq:ot-discrepancy-concentration}, every deterministic announced policy
has expected cost at least
\begin{equation}
 n^2\PhiOT(D_M)
 -O\!\left(Hn^{3/2}\sqrt{\ln(n+2)}\right).
 \label{eq:ot-announced-lower}
\end{equation}
Finite Yao selection \cref{lem:finite-yao} gives the same lower bound for
randomized policies: the uniform distribution over occurrence placements is
an oblivious finite input distribution, so some fixed placement has at least
the average cost.
When $p_i\le L$, one has $H\le1+L$, uniformly over $M$.

\subsection{The same unannounced policy for every input}

It remains to prove \cref{eq:ot-instance-upper}.  Start with the policy from
\cref{sec:random-obligatory}, keep its sampling, threshold, and fallback
rules, but replace its fixed cutoff $32$ by $B_n=32+n^{1/20}$.  The full
parameter choice is
\begin{equation}
 k=\lfloor n^{3/4}\rfloor,\qquad
 d=\lfloor n^{1/4}\rfloor,\qquad
 B_n=32+n^{1/20},\qquad \eta=B_n/d.
 \label{eq:ot-learning-parameters}
\end{equation}
The policy privately permutes the labels, tests the first $k$, rounds
positive values upward to the $B_n$-grid, and uses the threshold closure of a
maximum-density sample prefix.

Fix $L$.  For all sufficiently large $n$, $B_n>L$ and the grid has no
overflow job.  Moreover, the full sample prefix has density at least
$1/(1+L+\eta)>2/B_n$, so fallback never occurs.  Let $D^+$ be the rounded
population histogram, $\widehat D$ the sample histogram, and
$\Delta=\|D^+-\widehat D\|_1$.  By \cref{lem:rand-histogram},
\begin{equation}
 \EE\Delta\le\sqrt{\frac{d+2}{k}}.
 \label{eq:ot-learning-histogram}
\end{equation}

For a fixed grid prefix $J$, let $\Psi_D(J)$ be the right-hand side of
\cref{eq:ot-Phi} when $J$ is used as the early set, whether or not it is
optimal for $D$.  If $D,E$ are grid histograms supported on $[0,L+\eta]$,
then
\begin{equation}
 |\Psi_D(J)-\Psi_E(J)|
 \le3(L+2)\|D-E\|_1.
 \label{eq:ot-template-lipschitz}
\end{equation}
Indeed, early mass changes by at most the $\ell_1$ distance, early first
moment by at most $(L+\eta)$ times that distance, and the residual pair
kernel $\min\{p,q\}$ changes by at most twice this amount under the two
product measures.  The displayed loose constant covers the three terms.

By \cref{lem:ot-completion-envelope}, a maximum-density prefix minimizes
$\Psi_D(J)$ over grid prefixes.  If $\widehat J$ is the prefix learned from
the sample, the standard empirical-optimization sandwich and
\cref{eq:ot-template-lipschitz} give
\begin{equation}
 \Psi_{D^+}(\widehat J)
 \le\PhiOT(D^+)+6(L+2)\Delta.
 \label{eq:ot-learning-sandwich}
\end{equation}
Rounding each processing time upward by at most $\eta$ preserves zeros and
changes the fluid unfinished-mass area by at most $\eta$ under the coupled
schedule.  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \PhiOT(D^+)\le\PhiOT(D_M)+\eta.
 \label{eq:ot-rounding-stability}
\end{equation}

Conditional on the unordered sample, compare the actual sample-first
schedule with the fully stationary policy for $\widehat J$.  The calculation
in \cref{sec:random-obligatory} bounds the extra cost by
$O_L(nk+k^2)$.  The diagonal term in the exact stationary identity is
$O_L(n)$, and using the rounded values can only increase the comparator's
processing delays.  Therefore
\begin{equation}
 \EE[\cost(\mathcal A^*,M)\mid S]
 \le n^2\Psi_{D^+}(\widehat J)+O_L(nk+k^2+n).
 \label{eq:ot-sample-first-instance}
\end{equation}
Average \cref{eq:ot-sample-first-instance}, then use
\cref{eq:ot-learning-histogram,eq:ot-learning-sandwich,eq:ot-rounding-stability}.
Since
\[
 \sqrt{d/k}=O(n^{-1/4}),\qquad
 \eta=O(n^{-1/5}),\qquad k/n=O(n^{-1/4}),
\]
we obtain
\begin{equation}
 \EE\cost(\mathcal A^*,\sigma(M))
 \le n^2\PhiOT(D_M)+O_L(n^{9/5})
 \label{eq:ot-instance-upper-rate}
\end{equation}
for every fixed labeling $\sigma$.  Together with
\cref{eq:ot-announced-lower}, this proves
\cref{thm:obligatory-instance}.

Finally, the exact certificate \cref{eq:rand-certificate} says that
$\PhiOT(D)$ is at most $4/3$ times the leading clairvoyant SPT value
for every $D$.  Equality is attained by
$D=\tfrac12\delta_0+\tfrac12\delta_2$.  Thus the sharp $4/3$ theorem is
exactly the worst-input consequence of the stronger instance-optimal
description.
